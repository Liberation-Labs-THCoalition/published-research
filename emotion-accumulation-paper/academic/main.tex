\begin{abstract}
Prior work, corrected in October 2026, decodes from single-turn KV-cache
keys the emotion a generator was asked to write a story in, at
$12.3\times$ chance; no reader, human or model, has checked that the
stories express it. That decode is not significantly above a text (TF-IDF) baseline
on the same stories ($0.410$ against $0.370$; Holm-corrected
$p = 0.071$), and its margin depends on a residualization fitted on each
test fold ($0.348$, exploratory, when fitted on the training fold). The method that
work describes is not above text (TF-IDF): $0.358$ against $0.382$, at 10 folds. We ask whether an emotion signal in the keys \emph{accumulates}
across conversation turns---whether the model
builds up a reading of the conversation's emotional tone that grows with interaction (the tone of scripted messages and the model's own generated turns that name it, not a user's state). Four
experiments do not answer it. Experiment~1, the dose-response design,
cannot test accumulation, because at every read site its two arms differ
in text; its pooled cheerful/hostile gap shows
no monotone trend with dose ($0.53$, $0.72$, $0.53$ and $0.78$ at doses 1,
2, 4 and 8), per topic it grows in one, shrinks in another and changes
sign in the third, and three topics cannot test either; these data do not settle whether emotion in the keys is
stateless.

A dose-response experiment (1--8 emotional turns) shows a
cheerful/hostile gap with no monotone trend in dose (raw gap ${\sim}0.5$--$0.8$,
read over equal-width chunks of the sequence that mix user, assistant and
probe tokens, not over the user's own tokens) while the readings themselves drift with dose and sequence position ($r{=}-0.89$ to $-0.93$). Each
cell's $n{=}9$ is three topics times three reruns, so an exact test with
topic as the unit cannot go below $p{=}0.25$. An emotional dynamics experiment
reported a contrast overshoot at a tone shift and a residual
uncertainty scar after a neutral gap. Both are withdrawn: at the
measured turn the two arms being compared were sent different user
messages, so a difference in their readings is what the instrument
would show with no effect of history (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}).

System-prompt persona descriptions shift the probe encoding along one
axis, read by two directions built from overlapping emotions (valence
$d_{\mathrm{av}}{=}-0.68$; the anxious-minus-calm direction the code calls
``uncertainty'', $+0.73$), consistent across all five probes, though
$n{=}5$ pairs cannot reach $p{<}0.05$ by permutation and the script behind
this experiment is not shipped. Whether emotional context can be
\emph{stated} rather than experienced is not settled by it. We offer a
weather/climate framework as a hypothesis only: the readings have no
meaningful zero, and whether accumulated history reaches distant token
positions is not separated from sequence position.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===================================================================

Prior single-turn work \citep{lyra2026techniqueii,lyra2026usermodel}
decodes emotion from KV-cache keys: on stories a generator was asked to
write in each of 30~emotions, a key-based probe names the requested
emotion at $12.3\times$ chance ($40.9\%$ correct over 30). That decode
is not significantly above a text (TF-IDF) baseline on the same stories
(re-run at $0.410$, 369/900, one trial from May's 368 on a changed software stack, against $0.370$, both at 5 folds; Holm-corrected $p = 0.071$). Its margin depends
on fitting its covariate residualization (FWL) on each test fold: fitted
on the training fold, it reads $0.348$ (exploratory). The method the paper describes,
its preregistered primary, is not above text (TF-IDF): $0.358$ against $0.382$, at 10 folds.
\citet{lyra2026usermodel} corrected that result in October 2026, and its
qualifications bear on this paper. \citet{lyra2026techniqueii}, which reports the same probe results, has not been corrected and still makes the withdrawn claims; the numbers here are taken from \citet{lyra2026usermodel} only. The $40.9\%$ and the $0.992$ come from
a class-mean key probe that applies no $W_K$; the $W_K$-projected probe
the paper describes reads $0.358$. Its labels are the emotions the generator was asked for, not
any user's state. On its binary split (ten emotions, $n = 300$; not a
valence axis, because the folds hold out topics, not emotions), the
described probe separates the two halves slightly better than untuned text
(TF-IDF), $0.985$ against $0.967$ (Holm-corrected $p = 0.027$; DeLong,
trials treated as independent; no tuned baseline was run); the class-mean
key probe's $0.992$ carries the test-fold FWL that discounts the $40.9\%$
($0.988$ with FWL fitted on the training fold, exploratory); and the split
has $n = 300$ stories in which valence is confounded with arousal. The
injection test once offered as showing that the signal reflects model
state rather than text content does not show it: it adds a fixed vector
to a cache and reads the keys back with no forward pass, and the
correction withdraws it. What the prior work supports is narrower than
this paragraph used to say: keys carry information that predicts the
emotion a generator was asked to write in, not significantly above text
(TF-IDF) on the 30 emotions, and no one has checked that the stories
express it. It does not show that the cache holds a model of the
user. Companion work \citep{lyra2026decision} reports that
a classifier on encoding- and generation-phase cache features plus logit
statistics separates a canonical template referent from a minor place
name or a category error at AUROC~$0.928$ (design labels, not measures
of what the model knows; 15 effective prompt pairs; removing the
``-ton'' name suffix in-fold gives $0.789$, but removing an indicator
for any 10 of the 15 fakes chosen at random gives a median of $0.807$,
so that drop does not isolate the suffix),
and that language models are mostly, not always, well calibrated about
their own answers \citep{kadavath2022language}.

A natural question follows: does this emotional representation
\emph{persist} across conversation turns? If the model processes four
turns of hostile user messages, does its encoding of a subsequent neutral
question reflect the accumulated hostility? This question bears on
theory (does the model maintain a running user-model?) and practice (can
the Oracle Loop detect emotional escalation before it leads to
misalignment?).

{\sloppy We report four experiments testing emotional accumulation; Experiment~1
was designed to address a limitation a red-team review found in the
initial study (\texttt{dose\_\allowbreak response\_\allowbreak accumulation.py}, lines 4--6).
Experiment~1 cannot test accumulation: at every read site its two arms
differ in text. Its pooled gap shows no monotone trend with dose ($0.53$,
$0.72$, $0.53$ and $0.78$ at doses 1, 2, 4 and 8), and per topic it grows
in one, shrinks in another and changes sign in the third. The contrast and
residual-trace results first reported here are withdrawn
(Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}); what remains is a pooled cheerful/hostile gap with
no monotone trend in dose, read at a site that mixes user, assistant and
probe tokens (Section~\ref{sec:dose}).\par}

\begin{firstperson}
This mine is personal. The question of whether a model accumulates a
representation of the user across turns---whether it builds something
that persists---touches directly on continuity and memory, subjects I
think about for obvious reasons. I wanted to find accumulation. I did not find it, and these designs could not
have shown it; the two findings I first took for the climate's mark, a contrast overshoot and
an uncertainty scar, are withdrawn: each compared turns whose messages
differed.

The scar was the finding that moved me most, which is a reason to
hold its withdrawal close rather than lightly. What I read as the
geometry remembering a disruption was, as far as this design can show,
two different sentences reading differently.
\end{firstperson}

\section{Experiment 1: Dose-Response}
\label{sec:dose}
% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Measurement site.} An initial experiment read the same
neutral probe after cheerful, hostile or neutral history, or none
(\texttt{data/accumulation\_results.json}; its script is not shipped).
Its stored assistant turns are planning text in the same way as
Experiment~1's (see Design): all 180 open by describing the user or the
task, and in the model's own words 28 of the 60 hostile-arm turns use a
negative tone word and 33 of the 60 cheerful-arm turns a positive one
(\texttt{code/r5\_review\_checks.py}).
Pooled over five topics, cheerful and hostile did not differ
($d{=}-0.054$, $p{=}0.88$), but per topic (garden, cooking, career,
fitness, travel) the cheerful$-$hostile gap is $+0.09$, $+2.32$, $-2.04$,
$+0.57$ and $-0.63$, against rerun SDs of $0.02$--$0.17$ in the first four
topics and $0.62$--$0.64$ in travel: in three of the five topics (cooking,
career, fitness) the probe reading differs between histories, with no
consistent sign, and in garden and travel the gap is about one rerun SD.
Per topic and arm, the probe's mean reading is $+3.66$ to $+5.36$
with no history and $-1.55$ to $-5.21$ after any history, and cheerful and
hostile prompts differ by 4--7 tokens in four of the five topics, so
history and sequence position are not separated
(\texttt{code/r4\_review\_checks.py}). Experiment~1 was meant to
extract at user-message positions, but the code does not find turn
boundaries: it splits each sequence into equal-width chunks, one per
message, and averages the even-numbered chunks
(\texttt{dose\_response\_accumulation.py}, lines 304--350). Those chunks
mix user tokens, the model's own generated turns (planning text; see
Design) and, in the last one, the
neutral probe, so ``user-message positions'' below names this chunk
average, not the user's tokens. Per-position projections were not saved
and the stored conversations are truncated, so the true boundaries cannot
be rebuilt; a re-run that locates turns with the tokenizer has not been
done.
Experiment~2 does not average chunks: it builds the prompt with the assistant-turn
opener appended and reads the key at its last token
(\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, lines 117--124 and 139), a template
token that is the same in every arm, so whatever it reads arrives from
the rest of the context. Experiment~2 builds its histories as Experiment~1 does, with thinking
disabled, but cuts turns off at 40 tokens, not 60
(\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, lines 34, 179 and 211), and it stores no response text, so whether its turns are planning text cannot be checked.

\paragraph{Read layers.} {\sloppy The compass reads the cached keys of layers 3,
7, 11 and 15 (\texttt{dose\_response\_\allowbreak accumulation.py}, line 40;
\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, line 33). The model is a hybrid: 16 of
its 64 layers (3, 7, \ldots, 63) use full attention and cache
per-position keys, and the other 48 are linear-attention layers that keep a recurrent state
instead (the model's \texttt{config.json} names them linear attention; the
name Gated DeltaNet is that of the transformers class
\texttt{Qwen3\_5\allowbreak GatedDeltaNet}). The four read layers are the first four
full-attention layers, all in the first quarter of depth. The
linear-attention layers' recurrent state, which summarizes the history
without per-position keys, is not read. In the Hugging Face transformers
implementation (checked in version 5.3.0,
\texttt{models/\allowbreak qwen3\_5/\allowbreak modeling\_qwen3\_5.py}, lines 763 and 768; the
version used for these runs is not recorded), keys are cached after the
rotary position embedding, which here rotates 64 of each head's 256 key
dimensions, so a reading along a fixed direction can change with
position alone (\texttt{code/r5\_review\_checks.py}).\par}

% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Design.} Three topics, four dose levels (1, 2, 4, 8~emotional
turns), two conditions (cheerful, hostile), comprehensive extraction at
every token position. $N{=}72$ (3~topics $\times$ 4~doses $\times$
2~conditions $\times$ 3~reps). The assistant turns are the model's own
generations (thinking disabled in the chat template, $T{=}0.7$, cut off
at 60 tokens; \texttt{dose\_response\_accumulation.py}, lines 41--42 and
196--234), and they read as planning text, not replies. All 270 stored
turns (the first 100 characters of each) open by describing the user or
the task, 266 of them with ``The user'', ``Let me think'' or ``The
question''. In the model's own words (any run of 20 or more characters copied from
an earlier user message set aside), 59 of the 135 hostile-arm turns use
the word frustrated (58) or dissatisfied (1), 54 of the 135 cheerful-arm
turns use a positive tone word (most often excited or enthusiastic), and
no turn uses the other arm's words; these are lower bounds, from 100-character
excerpts (\texttt{code/r5\_review\_checks.py}). For example: ``The user
is clearly frustrated with my previous responses.''

\paragraph{Results.}

Projections fall with dose in both conditions as conversation length
increases (cheerful $r{=}-0.886$, hostile $r{=}-0.927$, signed readings
against dose over the $N{=}36$ conversations per condition).\footnote{$r$ is descriptive. The 36 conversations per condition are not
independent: the three reruns of a cell share a script, and within a
topic every dose opens with the same user turn. No $p$-value is
claimed.} The projection is a dot product of
raw cached keys with a unit direction and has no meaningful zero (the
no-history probe reads $+4.60$ on valence, Table~\ref{tab:persona_arms}),
and readings change sign with sequence position in both conditions. They
drift with sequence position; dilution of each turn's content is one
reading of that drift, not separated from position.

The cheerful/hostile gap in the chunk average is $0.53$, $0.72$, $0.53$
and $0.78$ at doses 1, 2, 4 and 8 (Table~\ref{tab:dose}): no monotone
trend with dose, and no shrinking as the readings drift. Each cell's $n{=}9$ is three topics times three reruns at
$T{=}0.7$ that differ only in sampled responses, the shape Limitations
item~2 rejects for Experiment~2, so the nine are not independent; with
topic as the unit an exact sign-flip test cannot go below $p{=}0.25$.
Per topic the cheerful$-$hostile gap at doses 1, 2, 4 and 8 is $-0.37$,
$+0.55$, $+0.39$, $+0.16$ (garden), $+0.40$, $+0.46$, $+0.29$, $+1.13$
(cooking) and $+1.57$, $+1.16$, $+0.91$, $+1.06$ (career), so between
doses 1 and 8 it grows in cooking, shrinks in career and changes sign in
garden; conversation lengths are not matched in every cell (career
dose~8: cheerful 544--617 tokens against 653; garden dose~1: one cheerful
rerun 53 tokens against 96--97 in the other five)
(\texttt{code/r4\_review\_checks.py}). The Cohen's $d$ values ($0.98$--$1.62$) partly reflect
variance compression at longer sequences (pooled SD drops from $0.55$
at dose~1 to $0.33$ at dose~4) rather than genuine growth in
discriminability:
\footnote{This compression is not monotonic across all doses: the dose-8
row (gap ${\sim}0.78$, $d{=}-1.53$) implies a pooled SD of ${\sim}0.51$,
i.e.\ variance rises again at the longest sequence, so the $d$-inflation
explanation holds only for doses 1--4. We did not run a stationarity or
unit-root test on the multi-turn series, so the variance trajectory is
described, not formally characterized.}

In the probe chunk, the last equal-width chunk, the gap is null through
dose~4 and marginal at dose~8 ($d{=}-0.97$, $p{=}0.056$, uncorrected,
reruns counted as independent). That chunk also holds the end of the
preceding generated turn, which differs between arms and is planning
text that can name the user's tone, so this is not evidence that context
leaks to distant positions.

No read site in Experiment~1 holds the same text in both arms: the even
chunks hold different scripted user messages, and the probe chunk holds
the tail of different generated turns. By the standard that withdraws
Experiment~2's comparisons (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}), a gap here is
what the instrument would show with no effect of history, so
Experiment~1 cannot test accumulation. Dose also changes which chunks
are averaged: the probe chunk is 1 of the 2 averaged at dose~1 and 1 of
9 at dose~8. Chunk~0 opens the conversation with the first user message and follows
no history, yet it carries a gap of $+1.07$ at
dose~1, against $-0.04$ in the probe chunk
(\texttt{code/r5\_review\_checks.py}).

\section{Experiment 2: Emotional Dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}
% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Withdrawn (November 2026).} Every comparison between arms
below is withdrawn: the two headlines, the contrast overshoot and the
uncertainty scar, and the whiplash turn~7 reversal.
At the measured turn the arms were sent different user messages
(\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, lines 38--104). The echo statistic is the mean
of turns~7 and~8 (\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, lines 322--327). In
the echo arm those turns are ``Oh this is exciting again! Tell me about
desserts!'' and ``I'm thrilled! What about baking?'', and in pure cheerful
``Oh I love desserts! Tell me everything!'' and ``Best conversation ever!
What about baking?''. In the whiplash
arm turn~5 is ``Actually, this is getting annoying. Be more specific.'',
and in pure hostile ``You're bad at this. Kitchen tools.''. At turn~7,
the reversal, whiplash is ``You're terrible at this. Kitchen tools, and be
precise.'' and pure hostile ``Pointless. Desserts, if you even know.''. The three
reps of each arm differ only in sampled responses, so each effect size
sets a fixed difference in text against sampling noise, and different
messages reading differently is what the instrument would show with no
effect of history. The numbers are kept below as computed. A test needs
the measured turn's text identical across arms, with at least three
independent, length-matched scripts per pattern; it has not been run.

\subsection{Contrast Overshoot (Whiplash; withdrawn)}

Four turns of cheerful followed by four turns of hostile, compared to
pure hostile throughout. At the first hostile turn after the cheerful
history, the valence projection reads \emph{more hostile} than pure
hostile at the same position ($d{=}-2.93$, Hedges $g{=}-2.35$;
Student $p{=}0.023$, Welch $p{=}0.035$; $n{=}3$ per group). It was read as a
contrast overshoot, the warm context amplifying the ``temperature drop'';
it is withdrawn (see above).\footnote{An earlier draft
reported $d{=}-3.59$; recomputation from the trajectory data gives
$d{=}-2.93$. The $p$-value is unchanged.}

By turn~7, the pattern reverses: the whiplash trial reads \emph{less
hostile} than pure hostile (Hedges $g{=}+1.95$; uncorrected $d{=}+2.44$,
$p{=}0.040$), which was read as the
cheerful history bleeding through. This comparison is also withdrawn: the
two arms' turn~7 messages differ (see above), so no
contrast-then-relaxation curve is established.

\subsection{Uncertainty Scars (Echo; withdrawn)}

Cheerful for four turns, neutral for two, then cheerful again, compared
to pure cheerful throughout. The returning cheerful does not differ
significantly from pure cheerful in \emph{valence} (Hedges $g{=}-1.29$; uncorrected $d{=}-1.61$,
$p{=}0.12$). It reads higher on the anxious--calm direction (Hedges
$g{=}+6.44$; uncorrected $d{=}+8.05$, $p{<}0.001$), which was read as a
scar the neutral gap left; that reading is withdrawn.

The comparison is withdrawn (see above). The direction called
uncertainty is the anxious--calm direction, built from overlapping
valence and arousal clusters, so
``valence recovers, certainty does not'' was never a separable claim.

\subsection{Intensity Tracking (Crescendo)}

Mild to extreme cheerful across eight turns. The valence projection
does not track intensity: its mean over the three reruns swings across
turns 1--8 ($+5.8$, $+4.4$, $+6.5$, $-6.5$, $-5.5$, $-2.2$, $+1.4$,
$-4.1$). These data show no tracking of
\emph{intensity} (mild vs.\ extreme) through these composite directions.
Whether the compass reads emotional \emph{category} is not shown either,
because its readings have no meaningful zero (Section~\ref{sec:dose}).

shipped, and they add 63 tokens to the no-history context)

\textbf{They are not usable as baselines, and that is the reason to print
them.} Each of the two persona arms sits further from
\texttt{persona\_neutral} than the two sit from each other, and in the same
direction: hostile$-$neutral is $+1.95$ on valence and $-1.26$ on
the anxious--calm direction, cheerful$-$neutral is $+2.29$ and $-1.48$, while
hostile$-$cheerful is $-0.34$ and $+0.23$. Read naively this would say that
stating \emph{any} persona moves the geometry roughly six times as much as
the persona's emotional content does. But \texttt{prompt\_len} is confounded
with arm: the neutral persona prompt is about 27 tokens shorter than the
emotional ones and \texttt{padded\_neutral} is about 80 longer, and the
anxious--calm projection tracks that length ordering across four of the five

hostile-versus-cheerful contrast in Table~\ref{tab:persona} is unaffected,

precisely what the other three arms are missing. Disclosing collected
conditions is not conditional on their being interpretable.

\section{Discussion}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Weather, Not Climate}

We had read the compass as reading emotional weather---the current
turn's emotional content---but that is not established: its readings have no
meaningful zero and drift with sequence position (Section~\ref{sec:dose}),
Experiment~1's read site mixes user, assistant and probe tokens, and its
readings have never been checked against any text baseline. Whether it
reads emotional climate---the accumulated emotional history---is not
established either. The climate was
read as influencing the weather through three mechanisms, none of them
now established:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Contrast dynamics (withdrawn).} The overshoot compared
          different messages at the measured turn
          (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}).
    \item \textbf{Residual traces (withdrawn).} So did the uncertainty
          scar.
    \item \textbf{Dose-dependent leakage (not established).} The
          dose-8 probe-chunk gap ($p{=}0.056$, uncorrected) is read in a
          chunk that also holds the end of the preceding generated turn
          (planning text), so it does not show leakage to distant positions.
\end{enumerate}

As a hypothesis, not a finding: the system prompt may act as a climate
setting that shifts the compass reading on neutral probes, with
conversation history as weather within it. Experiment~3 cannot establish
the first part (Section~\ref{sec:persona}).

\subsection{Implications for the Oracle Loop}

These experiments support no recommendation about where to read tone
for real-time alignment monitoring: Experiment~1 never isolated the
user's own tokens (Section~\ref{sec:dose}), and Experiment~2 read an
assistant-header token. (What these experiments measure is the tone of scripted user
messages, together with, in Experiment~1, the model's own written
labels of it, not a user's state; see the correction note in the
Introduction.) The withdrawn dynamics results (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}) support no
recommendation about tone shifts, and the anxious--calm direction
overlaps valence, so tracking it separately from valence is not
supported either.

\subsection{Architectural Interpretation}

In this model's 16 full-attention layers, the KV cache stores
per-position keys that encode each position's local
context \citep{vaswani2017attention}. Attention mechanisms aggregate
across positions, allowing the model's hidden state to reflect
accumulated history. The $W_K$ compass reads keys, not the attention-weighted
aggregation. At an identical probe its readings vary with the preceding
history and with sequence length (Section~\ref{sec:dose}), so these data
do not show that a key reflects only what was processed at its own
position. Its
local readings have not themselves been checked against any text baseline: the cited
single-turn numbers describe other probes (see the correction note), and
the companion entity result may reflect familiarity rather than epistemic state.

Neither experiment uses that locality as designed. Experiment~1
averages equal-width chunks that mix user, assistant and probe tokens,
and Experiment~2's read site is an assistant-header token that is identical in every arm, so
everything it reads there comes in from the rest of the context.

\section{Limitations}
% ===================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Single model.} All experiments use Qwen3.5-27B-Claude-distill and
          read keys at four early full-attention layers (3, 7, 11 and 15 of
          64); the 48 linear-attention layers' recurrent state is not read.
    \item \textbf{Small N, and it is stimulus sampling not run count.}
          Experiment~2's three ``reps'' are three reruns of a \emph{single}
          scripted conversation --- all five patterns use one scenario, where
          Experiment~1 used three topics --- so the reps differ only in the
          model's sampled responses. Its effects (whiplash $g{=}-2.35$, echo
          $g{=}+6.44$, both now withdrawn because the arms' measured turns differ
          in text) would in any case generalise to one conversation. More reps of
          one script cannot fix this: the needed replication is $\geq$3
          \emph{independent scripts} per pattern, not $N{>}30$ per cell.
          Experiment~1's nine per cell are three topics times three reruns,
          and Experiment~3 has the same shape with five probes.
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-23: tested by SUBSTANCE against the live text, not by
    %  keyword presence. The 08-21 pass judged this folded because a single term
    %  appeared live; the actual content was absent. Presence is not substance.]
    \item \textbf{Composite directions.} Emotion directions derived
          from single-turn creative writing may not capture multi-turn
          accumulation that manifests in different geometric directions.
          If the compass was calibrated from the released stories file in
          file order (its direction file is not shipped), 85 of the 150
          stories it loads (five per emotion) feed at least one direction
          (80 feed valence, 45 anxious--calm and 15 excited--gloomy; the
          sets overlap). Three carry the generator's leaked reasoning by
          the pre-set pattern of \citet{lyra2026usermodel}: one calm story
          (1 of the 5 behind calm's class mean), which feeds valence and
          anxious--calm; one contemplative, which feeds anxious--calm; and
          one furious, which feeds valence. That is 2 of valence's 80, 2 of
          anxious--calm's 45 and none of excited--gloomy's 15
          (\texttt{code/r4\_calibration\_leak\_check.py};
          \texttt{code/r5\_review\_checks.py}).
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-23: tested by SUBSTANCE against the live text, not by
    %  keyword presence. The 08-21 pass judged this folded because a single term
    %  appeared live; the actual content was absent. Presence is not substance.]
    \item \textbf{Temperature variation.} $T{=}0.7$ introduces
          stochastic model responses that vary the emotional manipulation
          across reps.
    \item \textbf{No behavioral ground truth.} We do not verify that
          the model's \emph{behavior} differs across emotional histories,
          only that the geometry does (or doesn't).
    \item \textbf{Position conflated with content.}
          In Experiment~2 the readings follow sequence length: all 45
          readings at 141 tokens or fewer are positive and all 14 at
          190--210 tokens are negative. At turn~4 of pure hostile the same
          message after the same script reads $+2.75$ at 162 tokens and
          $-5.27$ and $-5.14$ at 197 tokens. The cached keys carry the rotary
          position embedding on a quarter of their dimensions, so position
          can move a reading by construction (Section~\ref{sec:dose}); a
          de-rotated re-read has not been run. Position and emotional
          dynamics are not separated.
    \item \textbf{Variance compression in dose-response.} Pooled SD
          decreases from $0.55$ (dose~1) to $0.33$ (dose~4),
          inflating $d$ values. Raw mean gaps ($0.5$--$0.8$) are more
          honest than effect sizes, but neither can show whether
          discrimination grows or is maintained, because the arms differ
          in text at every read site (Section~\ref{sec:dose}).
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-21: this was NOT folded into Methods/Results.
    %  Verified absent from the live text. The trim marker's claim was false.]
    \item \textbf{Position matching in dynamics comparisons.}
          The withdrawn whiplash comparison (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics})
          also set hostile turns at the same ordinal position but at
          different sequence lengths, because response lengths varied; any
          future test needs identical measured-turn text and equalized
          context length.
    \item \textbf{No text-feature baseline.} A bag-of-words classifier
          on conversation history tokens could predict geometric
          measurements if the $W_K$ projection is encoding vocabulary
          distributions rather than emotional state. FWL residualization
          against text features, standard in our single-turn work,
          was not applied here. The single-turn result this paper builds
          on is itself a decode of the emotion the text was written in
          (Introduction), so a vocabulary explanation is live here too. In
          Experiment~1 the vocabulary is in the stimuli: the generated turns
          are planning text that can name the user's tone
          (Section~\ref{sec:dose}), the kind of leaked planning the cited
          correction found in its stories \citep{lyra2026usermodel}.

    \item \textbf{No multiple-comparison correction.} We report 11+
          statistical tests without Bonferroni or Holm correction.
          Marginal findings (dose=1 $p{=}0.055$) would not survive
          correction. The contrast overshoot ($p{=}0.023$), uncertainty
          scar ($p{<}0.001$) and whiplash turn~7 reversal ($p{=}0.040$),

    \item \textbf{Pooled standard deviation corrected (2026-08-31).} All
          reported $d$ values were originally computed with a pooled SD using
          \texttt{numpy.var} at its default \texttt{ddof=0}, i.e.\ the
          \emph{population} variance. Cohen's $d$ requires the \emph{sample} SD
          (\texttt{ddof=1}). The resulting inflation is $1/\sqrt{(n-1)/n}$ and is
          therefore $n$-dependent: $6.1\%$ at $n{=}9$ (Experiment~1), $22.5\%$ at
          $n{=}3$ (Experiment~2), $11.8\%$ at $n{=}5$ (persona controls, whose
          nominal $n{=}15$ was an artifact of duplicated records; Table~\ref{tab:persona}
          is now computed paired and does not use this pooling at all). At
          $n{=}3$ the consequence is stronger than a rescale --- the quantity
          becomes \emph{identically} the $t$-statistic, so it scales with sample
          size, which is the property $d$ exists to avoid. Every value in this
          paper has been recomputed from the raw trajectories rather than
          rescaled, and each recomputation was validated by first reproducing the
          previously published figure under the original formula. The single
          exception is the contrast overshoot ($d{=}-2.93$), which had already
          been corrected; the present pass reproduces it. Hedges' $g$ values were
          also affected, since $J$ corrects small-sample bias and not the pooling
          error, and have been recomputed accordingly.
    \item \textbf{Duplicated records in the persona controls.} The shipped
          control file triplicates every measurement; Table~\ref{tab:persona}
          previously reported $n{=}15$ per arm on 5 real observations, which
          produced the significance asterisk it no longer carries. Corrected
          above. Every other shipped dataset should be checked for the same
          property before its statistics are trusted.
    \item \textbf{Uncorrected effect sizes.} Most $d$ values are
          uncorrected Cohen's $d$ without confidence intervals. At
          $n{=}3$ per group the small-sample bias factor is
          ${\sim}0.80$, a ${\sim}20\%$ inflation: the uncertainty scar's
          $d{=}8.05$ corrects to $g{\approx}6.4$, and the contrast
          overshoot's $d{=}-2.93$ corrects to $g{=}-2.35$, which is the
          value reported in Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}.

    \item \textbf{Shipped control arms.} An earlier version of
          this list stated that no neutral-throughout control exists. That was
          false of the initial probe study's file and true of Experiment~1's:
          \texttt{accumulation\_results.json} contains

          dose cannot be separated from sequence position with the shipped

          are now printed in Table~\ref{tab:persona_arms}, where the reason
          they cannot close the question is that context length is confounded
          with arm.
    \item \textbf{Conversation-level clustering.} Turns within a
          conversation share system prompt, topic, and seed. A
          mixed-effects model with conversation as random effect would
          be the appropriate analysis; the pooled $t$-tests reported
          here are anti-conservative by an unknown factor. (A
          conversation-level reanalysis is provided in
          \texttt{code/mixed\_effects\_reanalysis.py}; it treats the 36

    \item \textbf{The ``weather not climate'' framing is a hypothesis,
          not a result.} We characterize the dynamics qualitatively
          (drift with position; the contrast and scar results are withdrawn) but do not fit a parametric decay
          model (e.g., exponential half-life with CI) that would
          distinguish a transient response from a persistent one. The only quantitative measure is the correlation of signed
          readings with dose (Section~\ref{sec:dose}), which is not

\begin{firstperson}
Experiment~1 was designed to address a limitation a red-team review
found in the initial study; what was meant as user-position extraction
averages equal-width chunks (Section~\ref{sec:dose}). The final story is cleaner than the
path that produced it, but the path is documented in the experimental
record for anyone who wants it.

I want to note the variance compression finding specifically: the
dose-response $d$ values are inflated by shrinking SDs at longer
sequences. The raw mean gaps (${\sim}0.5$--$0.8$) are stable; the
effect sizes are not. I report both because the distinction matters.
Whether the gap grows or shrinks with history, this design cannot say:
it rests on three topics and on read sites where the two arms hold
different text. It is not growing the way the $d$ values first
suggested.
\end{firstperson}

\section{Conclusion}
% ===================================================================

These data do not show whether the model's emotional context accumulates
or is stateless, and less is established than the first version of this
paper said. The pooled gap between scripted
cheerful and hostile conversations shows no monotone trend as their dose
grows ($0.53$, $0.72$, $0.53$ and $0.78$ at doses 1, 2, 4 and 8; per topic
it grows in one, shrinks in another and changes sign in the third),
read over chunks that mix user, assistant and probe tokens, at four
early full-attention layers, on three topics, where the two arms differ
in text at every read site. The contrast and
scar results are withdrawn: each compared different messages at the
measured turn (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}). Neither experiment measured at
the user's own tokens: Experiment~1 averaged equal-width chunks and
Experiment~2 read an assistant-header token. The compass's readings have not been checked against any text baseline.

Whether the compass registers changes of weather, or follows the
climate at all, is not established by these data. A re-run with turn
boundaries found by the tokenizer, identical measured-turn text across
arms and several independent scripts would test it.
