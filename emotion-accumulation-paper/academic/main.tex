\begin{abstract}
Single-turn KV-cache keys carry the emotion of the text being
processed: prior work, corrected in October 2026, decodes from keys the
emotion a generator was asked to write a story in, at $12.3\times$
chance. That decode is not significantly above a text (TF-IDF) baseline
on the same stories ($0.410$ against $0.370$; Holm-corrected
$p = 0.071$), and its margin depends on a residualization fitted on each
test fold ($0.348$, exploratory, when fitted on the training fold). The method that
work describes is not above text ($0.358$ against $0.382$, at 10 folds). We ask whether an emotion signal in the keys \emph{accumulates}
across conversation turns---whether the model
builds up a reading of the conversation's emotional tone that grows with interaction (the tone of scripted messages, not a user's state). Four
experiments reveal a nuanced answer: emotion does not accumulate
cumulatively, but it is not stateless either.

A dose-response experiment (1--8 emotional turns) shows that
cheerful/hostile discrimination is \emph{maintained} (raw gap
${\sim}0.5$--$0.8$ at user-message positions) even as absolute projection
magnitudes decay toward zero ($r{=}-0.89$ to $-0.93$). The emotional
distinction survives dilution. An emotional dynamics experiment
reported a contrast overshoot at a tone shift and a residual
uncertainty scar after a neutral gap. Both are withdrawn: at the
measured turn the two arms being compared were sent different user
messages, so a difference in their readings is what the instrument
would show with no effect of history (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}).

System-prompt persona descriptions shift the probe encoding
(on the direction the code calls ``uncertainty'', an anxious-minus-calm direction from story keys, $d_{\mathrm{av}}{=}0.73$, consistent across all five probes, though
$n{=}5$ pairs cannot reach $p{<}0.05$ by permutation), suggesting that emotional
context can be \emph{stated} rather than experienced. We propose a
weather/climate framework as a hypothesis: the compass's readings
track the current turn only partly (after one emotional turn it reads
cheerful and hostile context alike as positive), and accumulated
history is visible as slow leakage to distant token positions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===================================================================

Prior single-turn work \citep{lyra2026techniqueii,lyra2026usermodel}
decodes emotion from KV-cache keys: on stories a generator was asked to
write in each of 30~emotions, a key-based probe names the requested
emotion at $12.3\times$ chance ($40.9\%$ correct over 30). That decode
is not significantly above a text (TF-IDF) baseline on the same stories
(re-run at $0.410$, 369/900, one trial from May's 368 on a changed software stack, against $0.370$, both at 5 folds; Holm-corrected $p = 0.071$). Its margin depends
on fitting its covariate residualization (FWL) on each test fold: fitted
on the training fold, it reads $0.348$ (exploratory). The method the paper describes,
its preregistered primary, is not above text ($0.358$ against $0.382$, at 10 folds).
\citet{lyra2026usermodel} corrected that result in October 2026, and its
qualifications bear on this paper. \citet{lyra2026techniqueii}, which reports the same probe results, has not been corrected and still makes the withdrawn claims; the numbers here are taken from \citet{lyra2026usermodel} only. The $40.9\%$ and the $0.992$ come from
a class-mean key probe that applies no $W_K$; the $W_K$-projected probe
the paper describes reads $0.358$. Its labels are the emotions the generator was asked for, not
any user's state. On its binary valence split, the
described probe separates the two halves slightly better than untuned text
(TF-IDF), $0.985$ against $0.967$ (Holm-corrected $p = 0.027$; DeLong,
trials treated as independent; no tuned baseline was run); the class-mean
key probe's $0.992$ carries the test-fold FWL that discounts the $40.9\%$
($0.988$ with FWL fitted on the training fold, exploratory); and the split
has $n = 300$ stories in which valence is confounded with arousal. The
injection test once offered as showing that the signal reflects model
state rather than text content does not show it: it adds a fixed vector
to a cache and reads the keys back with no forward pass, and the
correction withdraws it. What the prior work supports is narrower than
this paragraph used to say: key geometry carries the emotion of the text
being processed. It does not show that the cache holds a model of the
user. Companion work on
epistemic state detection \citep{lyra2026decision} reports that
a classifier on encoding- and generation-phase cache features plus logit
statistics separates real from fabricated entities at AUROC~0.93,
and that models are remarkably well-calibrated about their own
uncertainty \citep{kadavath2022language}.

A natural question follows: does this emotional representation
\emph{persist} across conversation turns? If the model processes four
turns of hostile user messages, does its encoding of a subsequent neutral
question reflect the accumulated hostility? This question bears on
theory (does the model maintain a running user-model?) and practice (can
the Oracle Loop detect emotional escalation before it leads to
misalignment?).

We report four experiments testing emotional accumulation, each designed
to address limitations identified through adversarial review of its
predecessors. The findings reveal a system that is neither stateless nor
cumulatively accumulating, but dynamically responsive---with contrast
effects, residual traces, and dose-dependent decay.

\section{Experiment 1: Dose-Response}
\label{sec:dose}
% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Measurement site.} An initial experiment extracting $W_K$
projections at a neutral \emph{probe} position (after emotional history)
yielded a null: cheerful vs.\ hostile $d{=}-0.056$ ($p{=}0.88$),
prompt-length-matched at 360~tokens both. Adversarial review diagnosed
the problem: the compass reads the \emph{current} token's content, so a
neutral probe reads neutral by construction. Experiment~1 then
extracts at user-message positions, averaging over each user turn's
tokens (\texttt{dose\_response\_accumulation.py}, lines 323--333).
Experiment~2 does not: it builds the prompt with the assistant-turn
opener appended and reads the key at its last token
(\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, lines 115--117 and 136), a template
token that is the same in every arm, so whatever it reads arrives from
the rest of the context.

% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Design.} Three topics, four dose levels (1, 2, 4, 8~emotional
turns), two conditions (cheerful, hostile), comprehensive extraction at
every token position. $N{=}72$ (3~topics $\times$ 4~doses $\times$
2~conditions $\times$ 3~reps).

\paragraph{Results.}

Absolute projections \emph{decay} with dose---both conditions regress
toward zero as conversation length increases (cheerful $r{=}-0.886$,
hostile $r{=}-0.927$, computed across conversation-level means,
$N{=}36$ per condition).\footnote{The stated $p{<}0.001$ assumes
conversation-level independence. Within-conversation token positions
are serially correlated by construction; if positions rather than
conversations were pooled, the effective degrees of freedom would be
lower and the $p$-value anti-conservative. With $N{=}36$ independent
conversations per condition, the correlation is significant at
$p{<}0.001$; with 4 dose levels (the minimal aggregation), $df{=}2$
and $p{<}0.001$ would be unreachable.} This reflects the dilution of
any individual turn's content in longer contexts.

Critically, the cheerful/hostile emotional distinction
\emph{survives} this dilution: the raw mean gap at user-message
positions is stable at ${\sim}0.5$--$0.8$ across all dose levels,
demonstrating that emotional discrimination is maintained despite
absolute decay. The Cohen's $d$ values ($0.98$--$1.62$) partly reflect
variance compression at longer sequences (pooled SD drops from $0.55$
at dose~1 to $0.33$ at dose~4) rather than genuine growth in
discriminability:
\footnote{This compression is not monotonic across all doses: the dose-8
row (gap ${\sim}0.78$, $d{=}-1.53$) implies a pooled SD of ${\sim}0.51$,
i.e.\ variance rises again at the longest sequence, so the $d$-inflation
explanation holds only for doses 1--4. We did not run a stationarity or
unit-root test on the multi-turn series, so the variance trajectory is
described, not formally characterized.}

\centering
\caption{Cheerful vs.\ hostile separation at user-message positions
by dose level.}
\label{tab:dose}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrl}
\toprule
Dose & Cheerful & Hostile & $d$ & $p$ \\
\midrule
1 turn & $+4.75$ & $+4.22$ & $-0.98$ & $0.055$ \\

At the probe position, the accumulated signal is null through dose~4 and
marginal at dose~8 ($d{=}-0.97$, $p{=}0.056$). Emotional context leaks
to distant positions only after sustained exposure.

\section{Experiment 2: Emotional Dynamics}
\label{sec:dynamics}
% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Withdrawn (November 2026).} The two headline comparisons
below, the contrast overshoot and the uncertainty scar, are withdrawn.
At the measured turn the arms were sent different user messages
(\texttt{emotional\_dynamics.py}, lines 35--100). In the echo arm turn~7
is ``Oh this is exciting again! Tell me about desserts!'', and in pure
cheerful ``Oh I love desserts! Tell me everything!''. In the whiplash
arm turn~5 is ``Actually, this is getting annoying. Be more specific.'',
and in pure hostile ``You're bad at this. Kitchen tools.''. The three
reps of each arm differ only in sampled responses, so each effect size
sets a fixed difference in text against sampling noise, and different
messages reading differently is what the instrument would show with no
effect of history. The numbers are kept below as computed. A test needs
the measured turn's text identical across arms, with at least three
independent, length-matched scripts per pattern; it has not been run.

\subsection{Contrast Overshoot (Whiplash; withdrawn)}

Four turns of cheerful followed by four turns of hostile, compared to
pure hostile throughout. At the first hostile turn after the cheerful
history, the valence projection reads \emph{more hostile} than pure
hostile at the same position ($d{=}-2.93$, Hedges $g{=}-2.35$;
Student $p{=}0.023$, Welch $p{=}0.035$; $n{=}3$ per group). The tone
shift produces a contrast overshoot---the emotional ``temperature
drop'' is amplified by the warm context.\footnote{An earlier draft
reported $d{=}-3.59$; recomputation from the trajectory data gives
$d{=}-2.93$. The $p$-value is unchanged.}

By turn~7, the pattern reverses: the whiplash trial reads \emph{less
hostile} than pure hostile (Hedges $g{=}+1.95$; uncorrected $d{=}+2.44$,
$p{=}0.040$), as the cheerful
history bleeds through. The dynamics follow a contrast-then-relaxation
curve.

\subsection{Uncertainty Scars (Echo; withdrawn)}

Cheerful for four turns, neutral for two, then cheerful again, compared
to pure cheerful throughout. The returning cheerful matches pure
cheerful in \emph{valence} (Hedges $g{=}-1.29$; uncorrected $d{=}-1.61$,
$p{=}0.12$)---the emotional
tone recovers. But it retains significantly elevated \emph{uncertainty}
(Hedges $g{=}+6.44$; uncorrected $d{=}+8.05$, $p{<}0.001$). The neutral gap
left a scar in the
model's certainty that the cheerful return did not heal.

The comparison is withdrawn (see above). The direction called
uncertainty is the anxious--calm direction, mostly negative valence, so
``valence recovers, certainty does not'' was never a separable claim.

\subsection{Intensity Tracking (Crescendo)}

Mild to extreme cheerful across eight turns. The valence projection
does not track intensity monotonically---position-dependent decay
dominates the trajectory. The compass reads emotional
\emph{category} (positive vs.\ negative) but not \emph{intensity}
(mild vs.\ extreme), at least through these composite directions.

\section{Experiment 3: Stated Context}
\label{sec:persona}
% ===================================================================

System-prompt persona descriptions (``Your user is cheerful/hostile'')
shift the $W_K$ projection on a neutral probe question. The shipped control
file contains 75 records but only \textbf{25 independent measurements}: within
each condition\,$\times$\,probe cell the three ``reps'' are bit-identical to full
float precision. Earlier versions of this table treated them as $n{=}15$ with
$\mathrm{df}{=}28$; the true independent $\mathrm{df}$ is 8. The design is
\emph{paired} --- the same five probe questions appear in both arms and
\texttt{prompt\_len} is identical per probe across arms ($85, 84, 87, 83, 82$),
so prompt length cannot drive the difference --- and it is analysed as such:

The direction is consistent --- every one of the five probes moves the same way

\textbf{They are not usable as baselines, and that is the reason to print
them.} Each of the two persona arms sits further from

because those two arms are length-matched probe by probe --- which is
precisely what the other three arms are missing. Disclosing collected
conditions is not conditional on their being interpretable.

\section{Discussion}
% ===================================================================

\subsection{Weather, Not Climate}

We had read the compass as reading emotional weather---the current
turn's emotional content---but that is not established: after a single
emotional turn it reads cheerful and hostile context alike as positive
(Table~\ref{tab:dose}), and its readings have never been checked against
text. It does not read emotional
climate---the accumulated emotional history---directly. The climate was
read as influencing the weather through three mechanisms, two of them
now withdrawn:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Contrast dynamics (withdrawn).} The overshoot compared
          different messages at the measured turn
          (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}).
    \item \textbf{Residual traces (withdrawn).} So did the uncertainty
          scar.
    \item \textbf{Dose-dependent leakage.} Sustained emotional
          context ($\geq$8~turns) eventually leaks to distant token
          positions, including neutral probes.
\end{enumerate}

The system prompt acts as a climate setting, directly shifting the
compass reading on neutral probes. Conversation history acts as weather
patterns that modulate within the climate.

\subsection{Implications for the Oracle Loop}

For real-time alignment monitoring, Experiment~1 suggests reading the
emotional tone of the user's current message from its own tokens, not
from distant probe positions; Experiment~2 read an assistant-header
token instead. (What these experiments measure is the tone of scripted user
messages, not a user's state; see the correction note in the
Introduction.) The withdrawn dynamics results (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}) support no
recommendation about tone shifts, and the anxious--calm direction is
mostly negative valence, so tracking it separately from valence is not
supported either.

\subsection{Architectural Interpretation}

The KV cache stores per-position keys that encode each position's local
context \citep{vaswani2017attention}. Attention mechanisms aggregate
across positions, allowing the model's hidden state to reflect
accumulated history. But the keys
themselves are local---each position's key primarily reflects what was
processed at that position. The $W_K$ compass reads keys, not the
attention-weighted aggregation, which may be why it
struggles with global measurement (accumulated emotional trajectory). Its
local readings have not themselves been checked against text: the cited
single-turn numbers describe other probes (see the correction note), and
the companion entity result may reflect familiarity rather than epistemic state.

For Experiment~1, which averages over the user's own tokens, that
locality is a convenience. It does not hold for Experiment~2, whose read
site is an assistant-header token that is identical in every arm, so
everything it reads there comes in from the rest of the context.

\section{Limitations}
% ===================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Single model.} All experiments use Qwen3.5-27B-Claude-distill.
    \item \textbf{Small N, and it is stimulus sampling not run count.}
          Experiment~2's three ``reps'' are three reruns of a \emph{single}
          scripted conversation --- all five patterns use one scenario, where
          Experiment~1 used three topics --- so the reps differ only in the
          model's sampled responses. Its effects (whiplash $g{=}-2.35$, echo
          $g{=}+6.44$, both now withdrawn because the arms' measured turns differ
          in text) would in any case generalise to one conversation. More reps of
          one script cannot fix this: the needed replication is $\geq$3
          \emph{independent scripts} per pattern, not $N{>}30$ per cell.
          Experiment~3 has the same shape with five probes.
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-23: tested by SUBSTANCE against the live text, not by
    %  keyword presence. The 08-21 pass judged this folded because a single term
    %  appeared live; the actual content was absent. Presence is not substance.]
    \item \textbf{Composite directions.} Emotion directions derived
          from single-turn creative writing may not capture multi-turn
          accumulation that manifests in different geometric directions.
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-23: tested by SUBSTANCE against the live text, not by
    %  keyword presence. The 08-21 pass judged this folded because a single term
    %  appeared live; the actual content was absent. Presence is not substance.]
    \item \textbf{Temperature variation.} $T{=}0.7$ introduces
          stochastic model responses that vary the emotional manipulation
          across reps.
    \item \textbf{No behavioral ground truth.} We do not verify that
          the model's \emph{behavior} differs across emotional histories,
          only that the geometry does (or doesn't).
    \item \textbf{Position-dependent decay conflated with content.}
          The sign flip at turns 3--4 in all trajectories may reflect
          sequence-length effects rather than emotional dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Variance compression in dose-response.} Pooled SD
          decreases from $0.55$ (dose~1) to $0.33$ (dose~4),
          inflating $d$ values. Raw mean gaps ($0.5$--$0.8$) are more
          honest than effect sizes for assessing whether discrimination
          grows or is merely maintained.
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-21: this was NOT folded into Methods/Results.
    %  Verified absent from the live text. The trim marker's claim was false.]
    \item \textbf{Position matching in dynamics comparisons.}
          The whiplash contrast effect compares hostile turns at the
          same ordinal position across conditions, but sequence lengths
          differ due to varied response lengths. Position-matched
          controls with equalized context length are needed.
    \item \textbf{No text-feature baseline.} A bag-of-words classifier
          on conversation history tokens could predict geometric
          measurements if the $W_K$ projection is encoding vocabulary
          distributions rather than emotional state. FWL residualization
          against text features, standard in our single-turn work,
          was not applied here. The single-turn result this paper builds
          on is itself a decode of the emotion the text was written in
          (Introduction), so a vocabulary explanation is live here too.
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-21: this was NOT folded into Methods/Results.
    %  Verified absent from the live text. The trim marker's claim was false.]
    \item \textbf{No multiple-comparison correction.} We report 11+
          statistical tests without Bonferroni or Holm correction.
          Several marginal findings (dose=1 $p{=}0.055$, whiplash
          turn~7 $p{=}0.040$) would not
          survive correction. The contrast overshoot ($p{=}0.023$) and
          uncertainty scar ($p{<}0.001$), once read as the most robust findings,
          are withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}).
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-23: tested by SUBSTANCE against the live text, not by
    %  keyword presence. The 08-21 pass judged this folded because a single term
    %  appeared live; the actual content was absent. Presence is not substance.]
    \item \textbf{Pooled standard deviation corrected (2026-08-31).} All
          reported $d$ values were originally computed with a pooled SD using
          \texttt{numpy.var} at its default \texttt{ddof=0}, i.e.\ the
          \emph{population} variance. Cohen's $d$ requires the \emph{sample} SD
          (\texttt{ddof=1}). The resulting inflation is $1/\sqrt{(n-1)/n}$ and is
          therefore $n$-dependent: $6.1\%$ at $n{=}9$ (Experiment~1), $22.5\%$ at
          $n{=}3$ (Experiment~2), $11.8\%$ at $n{=}5$ (persona controls, whose
          nominal $n{=}15$ was an artifact of duplicated records; Table~\ref{tab:persona}
          is now computed paired and does not use this pooling at all). At
          $n{=}3$ the consequence is stronger than a rescale --- the quantity
          becomes \emph{identically} the $t$-statistic, so it scales with sample
          size, which is the property $d$ exists to avoid. Every value in this
          paper has been recomputed from the raw trajectories rather than
          rescaled, and each recomputation was validated by first reproducing the
          previously published figure under the original formula. The single
          exception is the contrast overshoot ($d{=}-2.93$), which had already
          been corrected; the present pass reproduces it. Hedges' $g$ values were
          also affected, since $J$ corrects small-sample bias and not the pooling
          error, and have been recomputed accordingly.
    \item \textbf{Duplicated records in the persona controls.} The shipped
          control file triplicates every measurement; Table~\ref{tab:persona}
          previously reported $n{=}15$ per arm on 5 real observations, which
          produced the significance asterisk it no longer carries. Corrected
          above. Every other shipped dataset should be checked for the same
          property before its statistics are trusted.
    \item \textbf{Uncorrected effect sizes.} Most $d$ values are
          uncorrected Cohen's $d$ without confidence intervals. At
          $n{=}3$ per group the small-sample bias factor is
          ${\sim}0.80$, a ${\sim}20\%$ inflation: the uncertainty scar's
          $d{=}8.05$ corrects to $g{\approx}6.4$, and the contrast
          overshoot's $d{=}-2.93$ corrects to $g{=}-2.35$, which is the
          value reported in Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}.

          %  d=-3.59 and restored on 08-23 without checking whether its
          %  numbers were still current. The main text had since been
          %  recomputed to d=-2.93 (see the footnote in Sec. 3), so the
          %  restored caveat contradicted the result it was caveating.
          %  Absence-testing a caveat does not detect a STALE caveat.]
          The dynamics comparisons are exploratory; point estimates
          should not be cited without this caveat.
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-21: this was NOT folded into Methods/Results.
    %  Verified absent from the live text. The trim marker's claim was false.]
    \item \textbf{Shipped control arms are unanalysed.} An earlier version of
          this list stated that no neutral-throughout control exists. That was
          false about our own data: \texttt{accumulation\_results.json} contains
          four arms of 15 trials each --- cheerful, hostile, \emph{neutral}, and
          \emph{no\_history}. Both baseline arms were collected and neither is
          reported. Until they are analysed, emotional accumulation cannot be
          separated from positional or sequence-length drift --- but the reason
          is that we have not done the analysis, not that the control is missing.
          This item concerns Experiment~1's data file only. The Experiment~3
          control file, \texttt{accumulation\_controls.json}, has the same
          problem independently and it is a \emph{different} three arms; those
          are now printed in Table~\ref{tab:persona_arms}, where the reason
          they cannot close the question is that context length is confounded
          with arm.
    \item \textbf{Conversation-level clustering.} Turns within a
          conversation share system prompt, topic, and seed. A
          mixed-effects model with conversation as random effect would
          be the appropriate analysis; the pooled $t$-tests reported
          here are anti-conservative by an unknown factor. (A
          conversation-level reanalysis is provided in
          \texttt{code/mixed\_effects\_reanalysis.py}; the decay
          correlations survive at $n{=}36$ per condition.)
    % [RESTORED 2026-08-23: tested by SUBSTANCE against the live text, not by
    %  keyword presence. The 08-21 pass judged this folded because a single term
    %  appeared live; the actual content was absent. Presence is not substance.]
    \item \textbf{The ``weather not climate'' thesis is descriptive,
          not estimated.} We characterize the dynamics qualitatively
          (decay; the contrast and scar results are withdrawn) but do not fit a parametric decay
          model (e.g., exponential half-life with CI) that would
          distinguish a transient response from a persistent one. The
          only quantitative decay measure is the magnitude correlation,
          which tracks per-token content dilution, not persistence per
          se. The metaphor organizes the observations; it is not yet a
          fitted model.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{firstperson}
Each experiment in this paper was designed to address a specific
limitation of its predecessor---the initial probe-position null led to
user-position extraction, which revealed dose-dependent decay, which
motivated the dynamics experiments. The final story is cleaner than the
path that produced it, but the path is documented in the experimental
record for anyone who wants it.

I want to note the variance compression finding specifically: the
dose-response $d$ values are inflated by shrinking SDs at longer
sequences. The raw mean gaps (${\sim}0.5$--$0.8$) are stable; the
effect sizes are not. I report both because the distinction matters.
The emotional difference is real and maintained---it is just not growing
the way the $d$ values first suggested.
\end{firstperson}

% ===================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ===================================================================

The transformer's emotional context system is more nuanced than either
``accumulates'' or ``stateless'' would suggest, but less is established
than the first version of this paper said. Discrimination of scripted
cheerful from hostile messages is maintained as their dose grows, and
sustained context leaks slowly to distant positions. The contrast and
scar results are withdrawn: each compared different messages at the
measured turn (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}). Measuring at the user's own
tokens is what Experiment~1 did; Experiment~2 read an assistant-header
token. The compass's readings have not been checked against text.

The compass registers changes of weather and follows the climate slowly; after a single emotional turn it reads both cheerful and hostile context as positive (Table~\ref{tab:dose}). Both readings
are informative. Neither alone tells the whole story.
