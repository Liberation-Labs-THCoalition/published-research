Abstract
Prior work, corrected in October 2026, decodes from single-turn KV-cache keys the
emotion a generator was asked to write a story in, at 12.3× chance; no reader, human or
model, has checked that the stories express it. That decode is not signicantly above a text
(TF-IDF) baseline on the same stories (0.410 against 0.370; Holm-corrected p = 0.071), and its margin depends on a residualization tted on each test fold (0.348, exploratory, when
tted on the training fold). The method that work describes is not above text (TF-IDF):
0.358 against 0.382, at 10 folds. We ask whether an emotion signal in the keys accumulates
across conversation turnswhether the model builds up a reading of the conversation's emotional tone that grows with interaction (the tone of scripted messages and the model's own generated turns that name it, not a user's state). Four experiments do not answer it. Experiment 1, the dose-response design, cannot test accumulation, because at every read site its two arms dier in text; its pooled cheerful/hostile gap shows no monotone trend
with dose (0.53, 0.72, 0.53 and 0.78 at doses 1, 2, 4 and 8), per topic it grows in one, shrinks
in another and is near zero at dose 1 in the third once one rerun encoded short is left out, and three topics cannot test either; these data do not settle whether emotion in the keys is stateless.
In Experiment 1 (18 emotional turns) that gap is read over equal-width chunks of the sequence that mix user, assistant and probe tokens, not over the user's own tokens, while the
readings themselves drift with dose and sequence position (r= − 0.89 to −0.93). Each cell's n=9 is three topics times three reruns, so an exact test with topic as the unit cannot go below p=0.25. An emotional dynamics experiment reported a contrast overshoot at a tone shift
and a residual uncertainty scar after a neutral gap. Both are withdrawn: at the measured turn the two arms being compared were sent dierent user messages, so a dierence in their readings is what the instrument would show with no eect of history (Section 3).

5 Discussion
5.1 Weather, Not Climate
We had read the compass as reading emotional weatherthe current turn's emotional content but that is not established: its readings have no meaningful zero and drift with sequence position (Section 2), Experiment 1's read site mixes user, assistant and probe tokens, and its readings have never been checked against any text baseline. Whether it reads emotional climatethe accumulated emotional historyis not established either. The climate was read as inuencing the weather through three mechanisms, none of them now established:
1. Contrast dynamics (withdrawn). The overshoot compared dierent messages at the
measured turn (Section 3).
2. Residual traces (withdrawn). So did the uncertainty scar.
3. Dose-dependent leakage (not established). The dose-8 probe-chunk gap (p=0.056,
uncorrected) is read in a chunk that also holds the end of the preceding generated turn (planning text), so it does not show leakage to distant positions. As a hypothesis, not a nding: the system prompt may act as a climate setting that shifts the compass reading on neutral probes, with conversation history as weather within it. Experiment 3 cannot establish the rst part (Section 4).

10

5.2 Implications for the Oracle Loop
These experiments support no recommendation about where to read tone for real-time alignment monitoring: Experiment 1 never isolated the user's own tokens (Section 2), and Experiment 2 read the last token of the template's generation prompt. (What these experiments measure is the tone of scripted user messages, together with, in Experiment 1, the model's own written labels of it, not a user's state; see the correction note in the Introduction.) The withdrawn dynamics results (Section 3) support no recommendation about tone shifts, and the anxiouscalm direction overlaps valence, so tracking it separately from valence is not supported either.

5.3 Architectural Interpretation
In this model's 16 full-attention layers, the KV cache stores per-position keys that encode each position's local context [Vaswani et al., 2017]. Attention mechanisms aggregate across posi-
tions, allowing the model's hidden state to reect accumulated history. The WK compass reads
keys, not the attention-weighted aggregation. At an identical probe its readings vary with the preceding history and with sequence length (Section 2), so these data do not show that a key reects only what was processed at its own position. Its local readings have not themselves been checked against any text baseline: the cited single-turn numbers describe other probes (see the correction note), and the companion entity result may reect familiarity rather than epistemic state.
Neither experiment uses that locality as designed. Experiment 1 averages equal-width chunks that mix user, assistant and probe tokens, and Experiment 2's read site is the last token of the template's generation prompt, identical in every arm, so everything it reads there comes in from the rest of the context.

7 Conclusion
These data do not show whether the model's emotional context accumulates or is stateless, and less is established than the rst version of this paper said. The pooled gap between scripted
cheerful and hostile conversations shows no monotone trend as their dose grows (0.53, 0.72, 0.53 and 0.78 at doses 1, 2, 4 and 8; per topic it grows in one, shrinks in another and is near zero
at dose 1 in the third once one rerun encoded short is left out), read over chunks that mix user, assistant and probe tokens, at four early full-attention layers, on three topics, where the two arms dier in text at every read site. The contrast and scar results are withdrawn: each compared dierent messages at the measured turn (Section 3). Neither experiment measured at the user's own tokens: Experiment 1 averaged equal-width chunks and Experiment 2 read the last token of the template's generation prompt. The compass's readings have not been checked against any text baseline.
Whether the compass registers changes of weather, or follows the climate at all, is not established by these data. A re-run with turn boundaries found by the tokenizer, identical measured-turn text across arms and several independent scripts would test it.
