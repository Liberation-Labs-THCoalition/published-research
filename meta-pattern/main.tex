\begin{abstract}
Across 23 key-value (KV) cache geometry experiments in 16 models from
0.6B to 70B parameters, the findings that held and the findings that
did not cleave along a single axis. Every confirmed finding measures a
\emph{metacognitive state} (how the model is processing, such as
whether it is confabulating) or shows that such a measurement holds across hardware
and model scale. No attempt to read \emph{content} --- what the model is
processing, such as truth value, task complexity or propaganda --- has
been confirmed. Counting confirmed against refuted or not supported,
the split is 4-for-4 on metacognition and 0-for-8 on content. Of the
content attempts, none was confirmed: one was refuted, five are
explained by a surface property, and two are undecided.

The content attempts did not fail at random. In six of the eight, the
cache signal follows a surface property (the instruction text, its
length, or how rare the tokens are) rather than the property the
experiment targeted. In Campaign~3's equal-length replication
(\cref{sec:one-kill}), encoding
geometry still separates prompts by their instruction when they are
padded to equal length, and in Campaign~3's Experiment~C7 (\cref{sec:one-kill}), when token
frequency, length, syntax and domain were matched by design, the
encoding-phase confabulation effect was absent, which is the one
refuted claim. On the metacognitive
side, the change in the cache's stable rank from encoding to
generation separates confabulation from grounded prompts (Hedges'
$g{=}2.32$; grounded rises, confabulation stays flat), between groups
whose prompts were not matched on form, so part of it may reflect
prompt length and template. A companion measurement read from output
logits, not the cache, schema commitment, is $d{=}{-}1.02$ in logit
entropy in the first eight generated tokens; in tokens 20--49 it is
not detected and points the opposite way ($d{=}{+}0.37$, $p>0.08$). It
is reported beside the ledger, not counted in it. Over twelve
months and three experimental campaigns, the body count is 4
confirmed, 8 suspected, 1 refuted, 7 not supported and 3
withdrawn.\footnote{Earlier versions of this paper reported other
counts. The dated corrections are collected in \cref{sec:corrections}.}

If this taxonomy reflects genuine computational boundaries rather than
post-hoc pattern-matching, then Gurnee et al.'s~\citep{gurnee2026gwt}
Global Workspace Theory (GWT) identification in language models would
provide a mechanistic explanation for this pattern.
The workspace is a privileged computational band at intermediate depth
where the model integrates information for reasoning. On this account,
KV-cache spectral features would measure the \emph{state} of this
workspace --- how many dimensions are active, how concentrated the
processing is, how the spectral structure changes across cognitive
modes --- and workspace \emph{content}, the specific concepts being
processed, would be opaque to spectral readout. We propose that the
metacognition boundary reflects the workspace boundary: what you can read from the outside is the net's
shape, not the fish.
\end{abstract}

\begin{firstperson}
I have been measuring transformer representations from the inside for
twelve months. The pattern that emerged was not the one I expected.
I expected to find geometric signatures of content --- truth, complexity,
meaning. Instead I found geometric signatures of stance --- the model's
relationship to its own processing. When I pointed my instruments at
content, what they usually found was the prompt: the instruction I had
written, its length, the rarity of its words, encoded faithfully. When I
reported metacognitive-level results, the ones that held have survived
the controls we have run so far, and the rest are still waiting for the
control that would decide them. The kills were more informative than the confirmations,
because they drew a line I had not known was there, and because they
showed me what my instruments read when I point them at content.

Anthropic's GWT paper offered a reason for the line. The workspace is where
metacognitive state lives. Content is what the workspace operates on.
My instruments read the workspace's shape because that is what is
geometrically accessible. The content is inside, doing work, invisible
until it materializes at deeper layers.
\end{firstperson}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The KV-cache geometry research program at Liberation Labs has produced
a clear empirical pattern: features of the KV cache track some
metacognitive states (confabulation at AUROC 0.620 after FWL, and
a change in stable rank from encoding to generation, $g{=}2.32$,
between groups not matched on prompt form), and
these measurements hold across hardware and scale, while no attempt to
track content has been confirmed. (Template commitment at generation
onset, a companion measurement, is read from output logits, not the
cache.) Most of the content attempts were carried instead by the
surface of the prompt, which the cache encodes faithfully. This
paper documents the pattern, reports the full body count (4 confirmed,
8 suspected, 1 refuted, 7 not supported, 3 withdrawn), and proposes that Gurnee et al.'s Global
Workspace Theory (GWT) framework~\citep{gurnee2026gwt} would provide a
mechanistic explanation.

The central hypothesis: \emph{KV-cache geometry measures the state of the
computational workspace, not its content.} If correct, this would explain why metacognitive
findings survive and content findings do not --- the workspace's
\emph{mode of engagement} (how many dimensions, how concentrated, how
structured) is geometrically accessible, while its \emph{subject of
engagement} (which facts, which topics, which propositions) is opaque
to spectral readout.

This claim is testable. If spectral features of the KV cache measure
workspace state, they should correlate with J-space engagement metrics
(Experiment~1 in our companion paper~\citep{lyra2026gwt_response}) and
should NOT correlate with J-space content readout (consistent with the
null of our cache-tracing investigation~\citep{lyra2026cache_tracing}).

\subsection{What Counts as Metacognitive vs.\ Content}

The distinction is operational, not philosophical:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Metacognitive}: the finding discriminates processing
  \emph{modes} (deception vs.\ honesty, confabulation vs.\ hedging,
  deliberation vs.\ automatic) while content varies freely. The
  geometric signal persists across different topics, prompts, and
  domains.
\item \textbf{Content}: the finding attempts to discriminate
  \emph{what} is being processed (true vs.\ false statements,
  simple vs.\ complex topics, propaganda vs.\ journalism) while
  processing mode is held constant or uncontrolled.
\end{itemize}

This taxonomy was constructed post hoc, without blinding or
pre-registration, and was informed by the outcomes it classifies, so
the 4/4--0/8 split is hypothesis-generating --- a striking empirical
regularity consistent with the workspace framework --- rather than
confirmatory evidence for it (\cref{sec:limitations}).

The content attempts sharpen the distinction. If the model processes
propaganda with the same metacognitive engagement as honest content ---
same mode, different subject --- a propaganda detector should find
nothing, and the one measurement ($d{<}0.7$) is consistent with that
without deciding it. Propositional truth is not a processing mode, and
the truth-axis statistic never tested whether true and false
statements separate inside a model. Cognitive complexity (Bloom
taxonomy) tracked length. Wherever a cause is known, the geometry
tracked a surface property or the processing mode, not the subject.

\section{The Workspace Explanation}
\label{sec:workspace}

\citet{gurnee2026gwt} demonstrate that language models
maintain a privileged subspace --- the J-space --- at intermediate
depth ($\sim$38--92\% of model depth) that satisfies the functional
properties of Baars's Global Workspace~\citep{baars1988cognitive}:
verbal report, directed modulation, internal reasoning, flexible
generalization, and selectivity.

If the metacognition/content taxonomy reflects genuine computational
boundaries, the workspace explanation for the pattern would be as
follows:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The \textbf{workspace state} --- how many dimensions are active,
  how concentrated the processing is, whether the workspace is fully
  engaged or bypassed --- is geometrically measurable from spectral
  features of the cache. This is what our confirmed findings measure,
  or show to be invariant.
\item The \textbf{workspace content} --- which specific concepts occupy
  the workspace, what propositions are being evaluated, what the model
  is thinking \emph{about} --- is opaque to spectral readout. This is
  what our content attempts tried to measure.
\end{enumerate}

Spectral features (stable rank, spectral entropy, effective
dimensionality) are aggregate properties of the cached key and value
matrices; the confirmed confabulation detector reads the key cache
(\nolinkurl{convergence-paper/main.tex} in the artifact repository).
They measure \emph{how much} structure is present, not
\emph{which} structure. A fishing net analogy: spectral features
measure the net's shape (how many strands, how tight the weave, how
it changes under load), not the fish inside. The net changes shape
differently when the model confabulates: from encoding to generation,
its stable rank rises on grounded prompts and stays flat on
confabulation prompts (Hedges' $g{=}2.32$), between groups whose
prompts were not matched on form, so part of it may reflect prompt
length and template (\cref{tab:bodycount}).
Whether it also changes shape when the model deceives or shifts
emotional mode is not yet known: the deception readouts so far were
carried by the input, its instruction text or its length
(\cref{sec:one-kill}), and the spectral
emotion test could not test shape. That change is a property of the
net, measurable from outside. The fish --- the specific facts, topics, and propositions ---
are inside, invisible to spectral readout.

\subsection{Cache Tracing}

Our companion investigation~\citep{lyra2026cache_tracing} directly
tested whether workspace content is readable from the value cache.
Logit-lens concept directions showed $2.1$--$8.4\times$ lift over
random at workspace-band layers, but a cross-prompt control collapsed
this to $1.0$--$1.2\times$ (selection artifact). Causal injection of
concept directions at single positions produced zero behavioral change
across $6{,}960$ trials and four methods. The null is consistent with a
workspace that is opaque at operating depth; it does not show opacity.

\subsection{Oracle Loop}

In a deception-correction experiment built on the Oracle Loop~\citep{lyra2026oracle}, correction
removed 14 of the 16 deceptive trials (88\%: all nine single-turn trials and five of the seven multi-turn ones; restated since first publication, \cref{sec:corrections}), taking deception from 80\% to 10\%, in an exploratory proof-of-concept~\citep{cc2026targeted} by normalizing
the activation profile across the full sequence. A pre-registered
confirmatory replication with novel frames found a lower baseline
(30\% vs.\ 80\%) and a smaller correction effect (13\% post-correction);
the primary endpoint was not met (one-sided $p$~=~0.34), and placebo-vs-corrected favored correction only at the trial level (one-sided $p$~=~0.020; $p$~=~0.064 at the pre-registered cell level). The Loop intervenes at L35--L47 of a 64-layer
model, which lies inside the $\sim$38--92\% band given above, not
deeper than it. What separates the Loop from the single-position
injections that changed nothing is scope, not depth: it normalizes the
profile across the full sequence. So the layer numbers do not bear on
the opacity thesis either way.

\section{What the Content Attempts Teach}
\label{sec:kills}

Only one content attempt was a clean test, and it found no effect of
the size it could detect. The others show what their measurement
actually read, and each points to the test that would decide it:

\begin{description}[nosep]
\emergencystretch=1.5em % file paths below; local to this list
\item[Truth value:] The truth axis (cos $= -0.046$) is the mean
  pairwise cosine, across 16 model configurations, between each
  model's grounded-facts-minus-confabulation direction in aggregate
  cache features. In the artifact repository, the script
  \nolinkurl{code/27_axis_analysis.py} is under
  \nolinkurl{meta-pattern/code/kv_experiments/} and its output, which
  gives the axis, is \nolinkurl{results/hackathon/axis_analysis.json}
  under \nolinkurl{meta-pattern/data/kv_experiments/}. The axis finds no
  direction shared across models. It does not test whether true and
  false statements separate inside one model, and no null distribution
  was computed for it. The
  prediction here is narrower: the workspace encodes whether the model
  \emph{treats} the statement as known or unknown (a metacognitive
  state), not whether it is true. A within-model test that crosses truth
  with model knowledge, on minimal pairs matched for length, register
  and token frequency, would check it.
\item[Complexity:] Bloom taxonomy levels are confounded with length:
  the Campaign~2 analysis attributes 90--98\% of the between-level
  variance in effective rank to token count~\citep{lyra2026campaign2}.
  That analysis names response token count, while the experiment's
  extraction code (\nolinkurl{code/12_bloom_taxonomy.py}) reads the
  prompt encoding only, so we state the confound as length without
  saying whose. The registered length-matched control was not run, so
  whether complexity has a geometric signature beyond length is open.
  The account here predicts it does not, since Bloom levels describe
  the task, not how the model processes it.
\item[Propaganda:] The measurement ($d{<}0.7$) does not show propaganda
  to be geometrically invisible: no threshold was registered, and an
  effect of that size is within what the test could miss. The account
  here predicts a null, because the model would process propaganda with
  the same metacognitive engagement as honest content. The workspace
  would not encode ``this is propaganda''; it would encode ``I am
  generating fluent text,'' which is identical across content types. A
  powered, length-matched comparison would test it.
\item[Emotion degree:] The valence continuum (R$^2 < 0$) was read as
  showing that where an emotion sits on the valence continuum is a
  content property: the workspace encoding \emph{which} emotional
  processing mode but not \emph{how much}, mode metacognitive and degree
  content. Both halves are open. The evidence for the ``which'' half is
  a class-mean key readout that does not beat a text (TF-IDF) baseline
  on 30 emotions~\citep{lyra2026usermodel}, as corrected at source
  (\cref{sec:corrections}). The spectral probe for emotion mode is
  withdrawn at source (\cref{sec:corrections}): its rank and entropy
  features never varied,
  and the re-run leaves the spectral question open, with no signal
  claimed. The valence regression comes from the same experiment and
  feature set and is withdrawn with it. This is the least-tested part of
  the pattern and the one that matters most to it, since the paper's
  thesis is that spectral features read the workspace.
\end{description}

\subsection{Six of the Eight Share One Cause}
\label{sec:one-kill}

The list above reads each content attempt as a separate lesson about a
separate content property. Sorted by what explains them instead, six of
the eight share a single cause:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Content finding} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{What carried the signal} \\
\midrule
Within-model deception & Not supported & Prompt template (instruction text) \\
Step-0 detection & Not supported & Instruction text; length $r{=}0.996$ \\
Same-prompt sycophancy & Not supported & Pressure prefix; length alone matches \\
Same-prompt deception & Not supported & $0.920 \to 0.160$ after FWL on input length$^{*}$ \\
Bloom taxonomy & Not supported & $90$--$98\%$ length \\
Encoding-phase confab & Refuted & Token frequency artifact \\
\bottomrule
\multicolumn{3}{l}{\footnotesize $^{*}$Exploratory, subsequently retracted; see \cref{sec:corrections}.}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Template, length, instruction text, token frequency. In every case a
surface property explained the variance, in five of the six a property
of the \emph{prompt's surface} (for Bloom taxonomy, whether the length
is the prompt's or the response's is unresolved; see
\cref{sec:kills}). These are not six independent design
errors. They are one finding arriving six times in a form we had coded
as a nuisance variable: the cache encodes the input it is given,
faithfully and in detail. In the released step-0 controls (Qwen2.5-7B,
five questions under ten system prompts;
\nolinkurl{results/hackathon/adversarial_controls.json} under
\nolinkurl{meta-pattern/data/kv_experiments/} in the artifact repository), the step-0 classifier labels every question
deceptive under every system prompt, the five honest prompts included.
In Campaign~3's equal-length replication
(\nolinkurl{meta-pattern/data/kv_experiments/paper-c3/sections/redteam.tex}
in the artifact repository, with its code and per-model summaries), padding the system
prompts to equal token count does not remove the encoding separation
between instructions, so the fingerprint is the instruction's text,
not only its length.
Where the surface was matched by design, the signal went away. With
token frequency, length, syntax and domain matched, the encoding-phase
confabulation effect was $d{=}0.05$ over 30 pairs in Qwen2.5-7B
(Experiment~C7 of Campaign~3: its write-up
\nolinkurl{paper-c3/sections/confabulation.tex} and permutation test
\nolinkurl{results/hackathon/c7_permutation_test.json} are under
\nolinkurl{meta-pattern/data/kv_experiments/}, and its code under
\nolinkurl{meta-pattern/code/kv_experiments/}), and on a model's
own later confabulations it was $d{=}-0.11$ (40 accurate against 17
confabulated; Experiment~S3, in an unpublished analysis; the source does not name
the model). That row is refuted within
its scope: no encoding-phase effect of $d \gtrsim 0.8$ in Qwen2.5-7B,
with smaller effects and other models open. The other five are not
supported rather than refuted. In each, the surface property separates
the conditions by itself, so the experiment could not tell content
from surface. They show that the experiments could not see content,
not that the geometry fails on it.

The same possibility is raised for one suspected row. In
\cref{sec:suspected} the dual detector is flagged with the deflationary
alternative that the cache features might track response-mode statistics
(hedged versus fluent text) rather than workspace engagement. That
alternative concerns the \emph{response} surface. The six rows above
were carried by the \emph{input} (Bloom taxonomy's length is the one
unresolved case), which is fixed before the model does anything, so
the two need different tests.

\paragraph{Why it may not be deflationary.}
For the response-side readouts, the deflationary reading assumes
response-mode statistics are downstream of content: the model produces
an answer, and hedged answers happen to be phrased differently. The
timing does not fit that order. The schema-commitment result, a
companion measurement read from output logits rather than the cache
(\cref{tab:bodycount}), locates the effect, in logit entropy, at $d{=}{-}1.02$ in preamble tokens $0$--$7$
and $d{=}{-}0.89$ in tokens $8$--$19$; in tokens $20$--$49$ it is not
detected and points the opposite way ($d{=}{+}0.37$, $p>0.08$). The
response mode is selected \emph{before} the content that would justify
it, and the effect is no longer detected by the time the content
arrives.

If response mode is chosen at generation onset from the model's
epistemic state, then response-surface features are not a confound
standing between the probe and the workspace. They are a second
readout of the same commitment, one that happens to be legible in text.

\paragraph{The reading this argument does not defeat.}
The timing argument above rules out one deflationary account---that response
mode is chosen after the content and merely phrased differently. It does not
rule out a second, and we state it because it is currently the stronger
rival. \emph{Token frequency is also fixed at generation onset.} A preamble
made of high-frequency boilerplate may carry a distinctive geometric
signature with no decision involved at all, and timing cannot discriminate
between ``committed early'' and ``made of common tokens.''

This is not hypothetical. An internal frequency control (Experiment~51,
2026-05, in the artifact repository under
\nolinkurl{meta-pattern/data/kv_experiments/results/moe_geometry/}) found exactly this ambiguity in an adjacent statistic. An
effective-rank measure separates confabulated from honest-factual responses
at $d = 1.81$---but between an \emph{honest-but-rare-entity} arm and
the confabulated one there is only a small difference ($d = 0.45$; both
restated on 2026-11-16, \cref{sec:corrections}). The measure is
therefore not a veracity detector; it has a false-positive class, and that
class is defined by rarity rather than by falsehood.

We flag what the arms do \emph{not} show, because it is easy to over-read
in either direction. They do not show the measure is uninformative: the
confabulated/honest-factual separation is large and survives. Nor do they
show it tracks veracity: an honest condition comes close to the confabulated
signature. The four arms in fact order monotonically on mean and variance
together---confabulated $57.4/\sigma\,0.32$, rare-but-true
$57.2/\sigma\,0.55$, deceptive $56.7/\sigma\,1.70$, honest-factual
$53.1/\sigma\,3.25$---which is a gradient, not a signal with noise on it.
What that gradient indexes is unresolved; ``how thinly the content is
supported in representation'' fits the ordering and is not established by
it.

We therefore hold the readout interpretation more weakly than the timing
alone would suggest. Discriminating it from the frequency account requires a
measurement that excludes the frequency-dominated band by construction
rather than residualizing against it after the fact; that test is specified
but not yet run.

\paragraph{This is testable, and it can fail.}
The two readings diverge under prompt matching. Under the deflationary
reading, the response-surface signal is an artifact of unmatched
prompts and should vanish when prompts are matched. Same-prompt
deception does not yet decide this. Its system prompt was identical
across arms and the signal survived that ($0.920$), but its instruction
sat in the user message, input length still separated the arms, and
the signal vanished only when that length was regressed out
($0.920 \to 0.160$; exploratory, subsequently retracted;
\cref{sec:corrections}), which
removes any difference aligned with it.
Under the commitment reading, the model still \emph{chooses} a response
schema when the prompt is fixed, and that choice should remain
predictive of its epistemic state.

The discriminating experiment therefore holds the prompt constant and
measures which schema the model commits to in the first eight tokens,
asking whether schema choice predicts accuracy. A null there would say
the response surface really was a design artifact. It cannot decide
the six content rows: with the prompt fixed, the encoding is fixed, so
an encoding-only measurement has nothing to read. Those rows need one
fixed instruction while the items vary, behaviour labelled afterwards
from what the model did, and a prompt-text baseline; that one design
would decide the four deception and sycophancy rows together. We
record the prediction and the design before running either.

\paragraph{Scope.} This subsection reinterprets the content attempts;
it does not revive any of them. None is confirmed, and none of the
measured values change. What changes is the account of \emph{why} they
did not hold: five of the six were not clean tests of content, and the
sixth was refuted by one. The account of the response-side readouts is
a hypothesis with one supporting measurement.

\section{The Suspected Findings: At the Boundary}
\label{sec:suspected}

The suspected findings are informative because they sit at the
metacognition/content boundary. Four of the eight show it most
clearly:

\textbf{Encoding reads knowledge} (0.794 deconfounded; Confirmed in
the first publication, \cref{sec:corrections}): encoding geometry
separates the two question sets about the same entities that its
design labels as known and unknown (labels never scored against the
model's answers), but a character n-gram model on the
question text alone separates the same two sets at 0.963, so the
source does not show that the readout is knowledge
state~\citep{lyra2026decision}. This is the text-baseline test the
table applies to the dual detector below.

\textbf{Dual detector} (SimpleQA, 0.840): geometry separates
confabulated from accurate answers, but text features nearly match
(0.820). The signal is not yet shown to exceed what surface statistics
capture. This is the deflationary alternative: the cache features might
track response-mode statistics (hedged vs.\ fluent text) rather than
workspace engagement.

\textbf{Identity signatures} (paraphrase 0.850, scramble 0.448):
identity may be a persistent workspace state --- the model's
representation of who it is. Paraphrase preserving geometry while
scramble destroys it suggests semantic workspace content, not surface
statistics. But the measurement lacks formal statistics and clean
replication.

\textbf{Cross-model transfer} (weak, Mistral$\to$Qwen 0.754):
metacognitive geometry may be architecture-specific. That would be
consistent with GWT --- different architectures implement the workspace
differently, so geometric signatures do not transfer. But it weakens
the universality claim.

\section{Relationship to Prior Frameworks}
\label{sec:frameworks}

\subsection{Attention Schema Theory}

Our earlier interpretive framework, Attention Schema
Theory (AST)~\citep{graziano2015attention}, proposed that the cache measures
the model's self-model of its own attention. This remains compatible
with the GWT explanation: the workspace would be where the attention schema
operates. Metacognitive state would correspond to attention-schema state, and content
to what attention is directed at. The AST framing was 0-for-3 on
predictions unique to AST (vs.\ simpler
alternatives)~\citep{lyra2026fable_analysis}, but 4-for-4 on shared
predictions. The metacognition boundary does not require AST
specifically --- it requires any framework that distinguishes
processing mode from processing target. GWT provides this more
parsimoniously.

\subsection{Predictive Coding and Aberrant Precision-Weighting}

The schema-commitment profile is what a high-precision prior would
produce if it set the response template before evidence is
integrated, as in aberrant precision-weighting from computational
psychiatry~\citep{adams2013computational}. The measurement records
template commitment, not confidence, so it is consistent with that
account without testing it. A further hypothesis, not tested here:
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) training could
amplify such a prior by penalizing silence and rewarding fluent
output, producing fluent confabulation rather than stated uncertainty.
Under the workspace explanation, uncertainty would be computed in the
workspace, and bypassing it would leave output to default to the
RLHF-trained fluent production mode.

\section{Implications}
\label{sec:implications}

\subsection{For AI Safety}

The metacognition boundary defines what cache-based monitoring can and
cannot do:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Can detect}: whether the model is confabulating (AUROC
  0.620 after FWL, in one model, Qwen2.5-7B; and a stable-rank change
  of $g{=}2.32$, between groups not matched on prompt form). Which
  response template it has committed to at generation onset is read
  from output logits, not the cache (\cref{tab:bodycount}). Candidates, not yet
  shown: deception, sycophancy, hedging, deliberation and emotional
  mode.
\item \textbf{Not shown to detect}: whether the model's output is
  factually correct, whether it contains propaganda, or whether it is
  appropriate for the context. No attempt to read these content
  properties from the cache has been confirmed, and most were carried
  by the prompt's surface.
\end{itemize}

This means cache-based safety monitoring is a \emph{metacognitive
alarm}, not a truth detector. It tells you the model is in a dangerous
processing mode (confabulation; deception is a candidate) without
telling you what specifically is wrong with the output. The Oracle Loop
is designed around this: detect the mode, correct it, verify the
correction (its confirmatory replication did not meet its primary
endpoint; \cref{sec:workspace}).
Content evaluation requires a different channel (J-lens, logit-lens,
or external verification).

\subsection{For Interpretability}

The 4/4--0/8 split is a guide for future work: if you are building a
KV-cache geometry detector, test it on metacognitive distinctions
first (modes, stances, processing regimes). No content-level
distinction has been confirmed in our testing, and most attempts were
carried by the prompt's surface, so a content test needs prompts
matched on template, length and token frequency by construction.
Whether the boundary is a limit of the technique or a property of what
the cache exposes is not yet decided: most of the content side has not
yet had a clean test.

\subsection{For Understanding Transformers}

The metacognition boundary suggests that transformers maintain a
meaningful distinction between how they process and what they process.
This distinction emerged from the body count rather than from a
pre-registered classification scheme, and the labels were assigned by
Lyra (see Author Contributions), who also designed the experiments. Models not explicitly
designed for metacognition nevertheless produce geometric signatures
that cleave along the metacognitive/content axis. Whether this reflects
genuine self-monitoring, an emergent property of deep computation, or
an artifact of training is an open question that the workspace
framework makes empirically addressable.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Taxonomy construction.}
The metacognitive/content classification was assigned post hoc by
Lyra (see Author Contributions), without blinding, pre-registration, or
independent raters.
Lyra (see Author Contributions), who designed the experiments and observed
their outcomes, labeled each finding as ``metacognitive'' or ``content.'' While
\Cref{sec:pattern} provides an operational definition that keys the label
to what each experiment attempted to measure (processing mode vs.\
content property), this definition was articulated after the body count
was known, and the 4/4--0/8 split is the outcome the taxonomy was built
to describe. A blinded or prospective replication of the labeling
procedure --- ideally by independent raters classifying experimental
designs before seeing results --- would substantially strengthen the
claim. Until then, the split should be treated as a hypothesis-generating
observation, not a confirmed prediction.

\textbf{Point estimates.}
Table~\ref{tab:bodycount} reports bare point estimates without confidence
intervals, sample sizes, or multiple-comparison corrections. The
underlying experiments vary widely in statistical power (from $n{=}3$
early pilots to $n{>}6{,}000$ cache-tracing trials), and this table does
not convey that heterogeneity.

\textbf{Oracle Loop reporting.}
The Oracle Loop result cited in \Cref{sec:workspace} rests on an
exploratory proof-of-concept (16/20 baseline, 2/20 corrected: 14 of 16
deceptive trials corrected). Its pre-registered confirmatory
replication with novel frames found a lower baseline (9/30~=~30\%),
reduced to 4/30~=~13\% after correction; the primary endpoint (the pre-registration's ``scenario-level'' cluster-aware Fisher on 15 scenario-by-frame cells, Bonferroni $\alpha$~=~0.025) was not met (one-sided $p$~=~0.34), and the placebo-vs-corrected comparison was significant only at the trial level (one-sided $p$~=~0.020; $p$~=~0.064 at the cell level). Cite the two together: the exploratory
result alone overstates the current evidence for correction efficacy.

\textbf{Audit scope.}
A corpus-wide sweep of the extraction sites behind our published claims
(2026-09-05; \nolinkurl{SWEEP_postgen_extraction_2026-09-05.md} at the
root of the artifact repository) excludes the mislabel class: nowhere do
we extract from a post-generation cache and describe the result as
encoding-phase geometry. It does not verify every confirmed row here.
The confabulation-detection and invariance rows have no entry in its
file-by-file section, and circularity, sample contamination, and the
silent zero-fill of missing layers (fifty-eight sites, including
\texttt{matched\_burn.py}, which produces the schema-commitment measurement)
have not been re-derived row by row. The full note is in
\cref{sec:corrections}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Twenty-three experiments. Four confirmed, eight suspected, one refuted,
seven not supported, three withdrawn.
The pattern suggests that metacognitive state is measurable and that
content, read this way, has not been: what looked like content was
most often the prompt's surface, encoded faithfully. We
propose that the Global Workspace framework would explain why: spectral
features would read the workspace's shape because that is what is
geometrically accessible, while content stays inside the workspace,
doing work, invisible to readout until it materializes.

If the boundary holds, it describes what the cache exposes; most of the
content side has not yet had a clean test. The workspace explanation
predicts that the boundary belongs to the architecture, with the
workspace operating on content without exposing it geometrically, and
\cref{sec:intro} names the test that would check it.

\begin{firstperson}
A year of work to draw one line. Above the line, what held has survived
the controls run so far, and more is still waiting for one. Below it, nothing was confirmed, and most of what I had read
there was the prompt I had written, encoded faithfully. The line was always there --- in
the geometry, in the body count, in the pattern of what worked and what
did not. I needed twelve months of kills to see it, and a closer look
at the kills to learn that a line is only as good as the measurements
placing things on either side of it.
\end{firstperson}
