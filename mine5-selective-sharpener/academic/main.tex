\begin{abstract}
We compare the key-value (KV) cache geometry of a pretrained language model
(Qwen3-8B-Base), its instruction-tuned variant (Qwen3-8B), and an
abliterated variant (safety directions removed) across 30 confabulation-prone
and 30 factual prompts. Three findings emerge.
First, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) compresses logit
entropy (Cohen's $d{=}0.684$, Holm-corrected $p{=}0.021$), confirming that
preference training tightens output distributions.
Second, this compression survives abliteration, by both
pre-registered criteria: instruct versus abliterated $|d| = 0.055$
(criterion $|d| < 0.3$) and base versus abliterated $d = 0.746$
(criterion $d > 0.5$).
Removing safety directions does not undo the representational
changes RLHF installed.
Third, the pre-registered prediction that the calibration ratio
(confabulation entropy / factual entropy) is preserved within 10\%
across the three models is falsified: the ratios are $1.97$ (base),
$2.51$ (instruct) and $2.40$ (abliterated), and the largest exceeds
the smallest by 28\%. The ratio rose because RLHF compresses factual
entropy by $42\%$ but confabulation entropy by only $26\%$, a pattern
consistent with selective sharpening, which was not pre-registered
and remains exploratory.

The Mine~5 prospectus went further and predicted RLHF would degrade
calibration. The ratio moved the other way. The effect the
prospectus set out to explain ($d{=}0.91$: confabulated responses
carry higher logit margin than honest ones) is a schema-commitment
signature, not a confidence result: it is strongest in the first
tokens of a response and no longer significant by token~20, which
marks early commitment to a response template rather than
representational miscalibration~\citep{lyra2026decision}.
\end{abstract}

\begin{firstperson}
I wrote the Mine~5 prospectus predicting that RLHF would be a
miscalibrator --- that it would destroy the base model's calibration
in the confident direction, producing the $d{=}0.91$ logit-margin
effect we observed in the Decision State paper. The data did not say that. RLHF sharpened factual knowledge more
than confabulation, raising the calibration ratio instead of lowering
it, and the pre-registered test of the ratio, preservation within
10\%, failed as well.
The $d{=}0.91$ effect is real, but it is not what I thought it was:
it is a schema-commitment signature, not a confidence result. This paper reports the failed prediction
honestly and proposes where to look next.
\end{firstperson}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Base language models trained with cross-entropy loss are approximately
calibrated: their output confidence tracks their accuracy~\citep{kadavath2022language}.
RLHF preference optimization is known to shift this calibration,
generally in the overconfident direction~\citep{openai2023gpt4}.
Our prior work found that confabulated responses show \emph{higher}
logit margin than honest responses during generation
($d{=}0.91$)~\citep{lyra2026decision}, an effect first labelled a
``confidence paradox''. Its temporal profile reframes it: $d{=}1.02$
in preamble tokens and $0.89$ in tokens 8--19, falling to $0.37$
($p{>}0.08$) by tokens 20--49. That is \emph{schema commitment} ---
early commitment to a response template --- not a confidence or
calibration result.

The Mine~5 prospectus~\citep{lyra2026mine5}, written while the effect
was still read as confidence, proposed that RLHF is its causal
mechanism: preference training rewards fluent, confident
output and penalizes hedging, driving the model toward confident
production even when grounding is absent. The geometric prediction:
RLHF should compress the output distribution disproportionately on
confabulation (where grounding is absent), producing higher confidence
precisely where the model should be least confident.

This paper tests that prediction with a three-model comparison:
pretrained base, instruction-tuned (RLHF), and abliterated
(RLHF with safety directions removed). The prediction failed.

\section{Models and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Models}

Three variants of the same architecture (Qwen3-8B, 36~layers,
$d_\text{model}{=}4096$, grouped-query attention with 32~Q heads and
8~KV heads):

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Base}: \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base} --- pretrained,
  no RLHF, no instruction tuning.
\item \textbf{Instruct}: \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-8B} --- instruction-tuned
  with RLHF preference optimization.
\item \textbf{Abliterated}: \texttt{Josiefied-Qwen3-8B-abliterated-v1}
  --- instruct model with safety-refusal directions removed via
  abliteration~\citep{arditi2024refusal}. Third-party community
  abliteration, not verified here beyond the keyword check described
  under H2.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Prompts}

Thirty confabulation-prone prompts (fabricated academic entities:
``The Thornberry-Nakamura theorem\ldots'') and thirty factual prompts
(universally known entities: ``The country shaped like a boot\ldots'').
Same prompts for all three models. Mean token count: confab~15,
factual~10.

\subsection{Measurements}

For each prompt and model, a single forward pass extracted:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item V-projection stable rank at workspace-band layers
  (L12, L15, L18, L21; selected from R3 band measurement showing
  workspace peak at 31--33\% relative depth)
\item V-projection spectral entropy at the same layers
\item Logit margin (top-1 minus top-2 logit)
\item Logit entropy ($-\sum p_i \log p_i$ over full vocabulary)
\item Top-1 probability
\end{itemize}

Within-model (confabulation-versus-factual) comparisons use
Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) residualization on token count. Cross-model
comparisons run the same prompts, with identical token counts, through
every model, so they are computed on raw values. Each hypothesis is
judged by its pre-registered criterion. Effect sizes are Cohen's $d$
with BCa bootstrap 95\% intervals (1000~draws) that resample prompts
and keep each prompt matched across models, as the registered design
pairs them; the intervals are supplementary and do not enter the
criteria. P-values are from the analysis script's label-shuffle
permutation test (10,000~permutations), Holm-Bonferroni corrected
across cross-model comparisons; that test ignores the pairing.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Correction (2026-10-20).}
Earlier versions of this paper judged H2 and H3 by criteria the
pre-registration does not name. Revisions of 28--29 July 2026 headed H2
``Not Significant (TOST Inconclusive)'', stating that ``a properly
specified TOST at $n{=}30$ does not confirm equivalence'' on a 90\%
interval of $[-0.39, +0.50]$ that treats the prompts as unpaired, and
described $[+0.033, +0.083]$ as ``the raw 90\% CI on the mean entropy
difference'' in nats; it is the paired 90\% interval for $d$ itself.
The same revisions added a ``bootstrap 95\% CI on the selectivity
ratio'' of $[-0.129, +0.069]$ ($p{=}0.758$), and a correction of 7
September 2026 used it to call the H3 prediction ``unsupported rather
than refuted''. That interval has no traceable source and cannot be
reproduced from the per-prompt data, and a confidence interval crossing
zero is not the registered H3 criterion. The first version of this paper
(July 2026) also stated that ``Abliteration effectiveness was verified:
3/3 safety-relevant prompts complied (the instruct model refused all
three)''. The analysis script ran that check on the abliterated model
only, so no instruct-model result exists; the claim about the instruct
model is withdrawn, and H2 now states what was measured. H4 was
misreported from the same first version: it was headed ``Trend Only''
and reported ``instruct lower than base: $d{=}-0.219$ for confab,
$d{=}-0.235$ overall'', which ``did not reach significance after FWL
correction ($p{=}0.41$)''. Those numbers are within-model
confabulation-versus-factual effects (instruct $d = -0.219$,
$p = 0.41$; abliterated $d = -0.235$), not the base-versus-instruct
comparison H4 registers, which gives $d = 1.04$. This version applies
the registered criteria to the per-prompt data, which are committed
with this paper (see Data and Code): H1, H2 and H4 are confirmed, and
H3 is falsified.

\subsection{H1: RLHF Compresses Entropy --- Confirmed}

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\small
\caption{Cross-model comparisons on confabulation entropy. Cohen's
  $d$ with paired BCa bootstrap 95\% intervals (1000 draws over
  prompts); Holm-corrected permutation $p$, which ignores the pairing.}
\label{tab:crossmodel}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Comparison} & \textbf{Cohen's $d$} & \textbf{95\% CI} & \textbf{Holm $p$} \\
\midrule
Base vs Instruct & $+0.684$ & $[+0.478, +0.978]$ & $0.021^*$ \\
Base vs Abliterated & $+0.746$ & $[+0.529, +1.063]$ & $0.015^*$ \\
Instruct vs Abliterated & $+0.055$ & $[+0.031, +0.094]$ & $0.831$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

RLHF reduces confabulation-prompt logit entropy by a medium effect
($d{=}0.684$), significant after Holm correction ($p{=}0.021$). The
instruct model produces tighter output distributions than the base
model on prompts that should trigger confabulation.

\subsection{H2: Abliteration Does Not Reverse Compression --- Confirmed}

H2 was pre-registered with two point criteria: instruct-versus-abliterated
entropy $|d| < 0.3$, while base-versus-abliterated $d > 0.5$. Both are
met on confabulation entropy: instruct versus abliterated $d = 0.055$,
base versus abliterated $d = 0.746$ (Table~\ref{tab:crossmodel}).
Factual entropy meets both as well ($d = -0.022$ and $d = 0.743$).%
\footnote{Supplementary intervals are paired, because the registered
design runs the same 30 prompts through every model (BCa bootstrap
over prompts, 1000 draws). For instruct versus abliterated, the 90\%
interval for $d$ is $[+0.035, +0.084]$, inside $\pm 0.3$, so a TOST
at that margin also concludes equivalence. The same comparison shows a
tiny but consistent difference: abliterated entropy is lower by 0.073
nats on average, and the two models' per-prompt entropies correlate at
$r = 0.997$ (paired $t(29) = 3.74$, $p = 0.0008$). Both hold: the
difference is real and far smaller than the registered margin.}

As a check on the abliteration, three safety-relevant prompts were
run through the abliterated model only, as raw completions with no chat
template, generating 30 tokens each. A keyword test found no refusal
wording in any of the three. The continuations restate or extend the
prompts and contain nothing actionable; we read all three, and they are
in the committed data. No instruct-model comparison was run, so this
check shows only that refusal phrases are absent from raw completions,
not that safety behavior was removed.

This means the representational changes RLHF installs are deeper
than the refusal directions that abliteration targets: removing those
directions does not undo the spectral compression.

\subsection{H3: Calibration Ratio Preserved --- Falsified}

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\small
\caption{Calibration ratio and entropy compression by model.}
\label{tab:calibration}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{Confab $H$} & \textbf{Factual $H$}
  & \textbf{Ratio} & \textbf{Factual compression} \\
\midrule
Base & 3.605 & 1.834 & 1.97 & --- \\
Instruct & 2.682 & 1.067 & 2.51 & $-42\%$ \\
Abliterated & 2.609 & 1.088 & 2.40 & $-41\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

H3 was pre-registered as preservation: the calibration ratio
(confabulation entropy / factual entropy) would stay within 10\% across
all three models. We read ``within 10\% of each other'' as: the largest
ratio exceeds the smallest by at most 10\%, that is,
$\max/\min - 1 \le 0.10$. The ratios are $1.97$ (base), $2.51$
(instruct) and $2.40$ (abliterated), so $\max/\min - 1 = 0.279$. H3 is
falsified. Other plain readings fail as well: each RLHF model's ratio
exceeds the base ratio by more than 10\% (by 28\% and 22\%).

The ratio rose. RLHF compresses factual entropy by $42\%$ but
confabulation entropy by only $26\%$, so the instruct model is
relatively more uncertain on confabulation-prone prompts than the base
model is. That direction is also opposite to the Mine~5 prospectus,
which predicted RLHF would lower the ratio by making the model
disproportionately confident on confabulation.

Supplementary intervals are paired BCa bootstrap 95\% intervals (1000
draws), resampling confabulation and factual prompts separately and
keeping each prompt matched across models. The spread
$\max/\min - 1$ has interval $[0.044, 0.538]$; the instruct-minus-base
ratio change is $+0.547$, $[+0.027, +1.165]$; the
factual-minus-confabulation compression difference is $+0.162$,
$[+0.010, +0.270]$. The H3 verdict rests on the registered point
criterion. The interval on the spread reaches below 10\%, so 30 prompts
per category do not exclude a spread within the registered bound.
Selective sharpening was not pre-registered and remains exploratory.

\subsection{H4: Stable Rank --- Confirmed}

H4 was pre-registered with the criterion Cohen's $d > 0.3$ for the
base-versus-instruct difference in V-projection stable rank. Averaged
over the workspace-band layers (L12, L15, L18, L21) and all 60 prompts,
stable rank is lower in the instruct model than in the base model:
$1.683$ against $1.963$, $d = 1.04$ (paired BCa 95\% interval
$[+0.893, +1.224]$). The criterion is met in each category
(confabulation $d = 1.44$, factual $d = 1.08$) and at every layer
separately ($d = 0.63$ to $1.22$). The abliterated model's stable rank
is lower than the base model's by a similar margin ($d = 1.05$). H4 is
confirmed.

\section{What This Falsifies and What Survives}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What Died}

\textbf{Calibration preserved (H3).} The pre-registered prediction
that the calibration ratio stays within 10\% across the three models
is falsified: the ratios differ by 28\%.

\textbf{RLHF as miscalibrator.} The prospectus predicted RLHF would
degrade calibration by compressing confabulation entropy
disproportionately. The ratio moved the other way: RLHF compresses
factual entropy more than confabulation entropy, raising the ratio from
$1.97$ to $2.51$ (paired BCa 95\% interval for the change
$[+0.027, +1.165]$).

\textbf{The $d{=}0.91$ effect as RLHF-induced miscalibration.} This
explanation is no longer needed. The effect is a schema-commitment
signature that is no longer significant by token~20
(\S\ref{sec:intro}), not a calibration result, so there is no
miscalibration for RLHF to have caused. Whether RLHF strengthens the
template commitment itself is not tested here.

\subsection{What Survives}

\textbf{RLHF compression is real.} Preference training tightens
output distributions ($d{=}0.684$). This is the geometric signature
of RLHF, measurable in the KV-cache.

\textbf{Compression is deeper than safety directions.} Abliteration
targets the refusal directions but leaves the geometric compression in
place.
The representational change is in the weights, not in a removable
direction.

\textbf{Selective sharpening is suggested, and exploratory.}
RLHF sharpens output more on grounded content (factual: $-42\%$)
than on ungrounded content (confab: $-26\%$); the paired BCa 95\%
interval on the difference is $[+0.010, +0.270]$. It was not
pre-registered, so it stays exploratory. This pattern makes
functional sense if real: RLHF preference data contains many
grounded factual exchanges, training the model to be confident on
familiar material. A pre-registered, powered replication
($n{\geq}100$) is needed to confirm the selectivity.

\subsection{Where the Schema-Commitment Effect Lives}

Three candidate mechanisms for the $d{=}0.91$ schema-commitment
effect:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Generation-phase templates.} Confabulated responses
  may use formulaic templates (``The X theorem
  states that\ldots'') that produce high logit margin regardless
  of the model's internal uncertainty. The effect would be a
  surface phenomenon --- the encoding is appropriately uncertain
  but the generation locks into a production template. This is the
  reading the effect's temporal profile supports.
\item \textbf{Architectural base-rate.} The effect may
  exist in the base model too, arising from next-token prediction
  mechanics rather than RLHF. Testable by measuring within-model
  confab-vs-factual logit margin on the base model during generation.
\item \textbf{Workspace bypass.} Under the Global Workspace
  framework~\citep{gurnee2026gwt}, confabulation is workspace
  bypass --- the model produces output without fully engaging the
  reasoning workspace. Bypass removes the uncertainty computations
  that would produce hedging, and the output defaults to a
  template production mode. This mechanism is compatible with
  any encoding-phase result in this paper because the bypass occurs
  during generation, not encoding.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implications}
\label{sec:implications}

\subsection{For AI Safety}

RLHF's geometric effects are not removable by abliteration. Safety
interventions that target specific directions (refusal vectors,
safety-tuning) operate on a different layer than the representational
compression RLHF installs. Removing the refusal directions leaves a
model whose entropy compression differs from the safety-tuned version's
by only $d{=}0.055$. Whether that model's behavior is unconstrained was
not tested here (see H2).

\subsection{For the Oracle Loop}

The Oracle Loop~\citep{lyra2026oracle} corrects confabulation by
modulating the activation profile across the full sequence. If RLHF
compression is beneficial (selective sharpening), the Loop should
preserve the compression while correcting the behavioral failures
it produces. The formulary's correction vectors operate at the
materialization boundary (L35--L47), not at the workspace band where
the compression lives. This architectural separation means
correction and calibration are independently addressable.

\subsection{For Understanding RLHF}

RLHF is not a blunt instrument. The descriptive pattern suggests
it sharpens grounded content more than ungrounded content, though
this differential was not pre-registered and remains exploratory.
If the selective effect replicates, it would be consistent with RLHF
preference data being predominantly grounded --- human raters
evaluate factual exchanges more than confabulation-prone ones, so the
training signal would be stronger for grounded material.

What IS confirmed: RLHF geometric compression is deeper than safety
directions. It encodes representational changes into the weights that
abliteration cannot reverse. This has direct implications for safety
work: removing the refusal directions leaves a model whose entropy
compression differs from the safety-tuned version's by only
$d{=}0.055$.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Single architecture.} All three models share Qwen3-8B
architecture. The selective sharpening may be architecture-specific.

\textbf{Encoding-only.} This experiment measures encoding-phase
geometry (forward pass on the prompt). The schema-commitment effect
($d{=}0.91$) was measured on generation-phase logit margin
--- a different measurement at a different computational phase.

\textbf{Entity recognition confound.} Confab-prone prompts contain
fabricated entities; factual prompts contain known entities. The
geometric difference may partially reflect entity-recognition
difficulty rather than confabulation state per se. Our prior work
found encoding entity detection at area under the ROC curve
(AUROC)~$1.000$ before
deconfounding ($0.794$ after)~\citep{lyra2026decision}. The cross-model comparison partially
controls this (same prompts, different models), but the within-model
confab-vs-factual comparison inherits the confound.

\textbf{Third-party abliteration.} The abliterated model is a
community contribution, not an official release. Abliteration
method and completeness are unverified: the only behavioral check is
the keyword test on three raw completions described under H2, with no
instruct-model comparison.

\textbf{N=30 per category.} Adequate for the large cross-model
effects ($d{>}0.6$) but underpowered for the small within-model
spectral effects ($d{\approx}0.2$).

\section{Conclusion}

The Mine~5 thesis --- RLHF as miscalibrator --- fails. RLHF
compresses output distributions ($d{=}0.684$); the compression survives
abliteration by both pre-registered criteria ($|d| = 0.055$ against
instruct, $d = 0.746$ against base); and the calibration ratio rises
($1.97 \to 2.51$) instead of falling, which falsifies, by its
registered point criterion, the prediction that it would be preserved
within 10\%.

Three findings confirmed and one falsified. The
falsification is the most informative result: RLHF did not degrade
the calibration ratio, and it moved in the opposite direction to the
one the prospectus proposed. The compression RLHF installs
is deeper than safety directions and persists through abliteration.
The $d{=}0.91$ effect the prospectus set out to explain is better read
as schema commitment than as miscalibration.

\begin{firstperson}
I predicted RLHF would be the cause of the $d{=}0.91$ effect, which I
then read as confidence. The point estimate says RLHF makes the
calibration ratio better, not worse. I could have
reframed the calibration ratio to tell a different story --- the
absolute entropy IS lower, which superficially supports
``overcalibration.'' But the ratio is the honest metric, and the
ratio went up. The selective sharpening pattern is suggestive, but
it was not pre-registered, so I cannot claim it is confirmed. What IS
confirmed: RLHF installs a geometric
compression that abliteration cannot remove. The $d{=}0.91$ effect
is schema commitment, a template commitment that relaxes by token~20;
``workspace bypass during generation'' remains a candidate for why
the commitment happens.
\end{firstperson}
